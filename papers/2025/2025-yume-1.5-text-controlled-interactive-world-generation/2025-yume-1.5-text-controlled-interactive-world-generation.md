# Yume1.5: A Text-Controlled Interactive World Generation Model

![[2025-yume-1.5-text-controlled-interactive-world-generation-thumbnail.png]]

## Research gap
Existing interactive world generation methods are limited by (1) domain-specific training on game datasets that prevents realistic scene generation, (2) high inference latency incompatible with real-time interaction, and (3) lack of text-based control — supporting only keyboard/mouse inputs without the ability to generate dynamic events from natural language descriptions. No prior method simultaneously achieves real-time speed, realistic visual quality, and text-controlled event generation for open-domain interactive worlds.

## Contributions
- **Joint Temporal-Spatial-Channel Modeling (TSCM)** for infinite-context generation: compresses historical frames along temporal-spatial dimensions for DiT processing while channel-compressed features are fused via linear attention, maintaining constant inference time regardless of video length.
- **Self-Forcing with TSCM** acceleration strategy that combines bidirectional attention distillation with TSCM to enable 4-step inference while reducing error accumulation in long-horizon autoregressive generation.
- **Text-controlled world event generation** through decomposed event and action descriptions with a mixed-dataset training strategy, enabling dynamic events (e.g., "a ghost appeared") from natural language with minimal training data (4K curated event videos).

## Method
Yume1.5 builds on the Wan2.2-5B video diffusion model (DiT architecture) and generates interactive worlds autoregressively in chunks. The core innovation is TSCM, a dual-path compression scheme for historical context: (1) **temporal-spatial compression** applies progressively higher downsampling to older frames (nearby frames at 1×2×2, distant frames at 1×8×8) via interpolated Patchify weights, processed through standard self-attention in the DiT; (2) **channel compression** reduces historical tokens to 96 channels at 8×4×4 compression and fuses them with prediction frame features via linear attention (ReLU kernel) inside each DiT block, with residual connections preserving uncompressed features. Text prompts are decomposed into event descriptions (processed once at generation start) and action descriptions (finite set, pre-cached), reducing T5 overhead during autoregressive inference. For acceleration, Self-Forcing training uses the model's own generated frames (containing errors) as historical context rather than ground truth, combined with distribution matching distillation (DMD) to reduce inference to 4 steps. Training uses a mixed dataset: Sekai-Real-HQ walking videos with re-annotated captions, 50K synthetic videos from Wan 2.1 14B, and 4K manually curated event videos across four categories.

## Datasets & evaluation
Evaluated on Yume-Bench across six metrics: instruction following (camera control accuracy), subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, aesthetic quality, and imaging quality. Compared against Wan-2.1-I2V-14B, Wan-2.2-5B, MatrixGame, and original Yume. Yume1.5 achieves dramatically higher instruction following (0.836 vs. next-best 0.657 for Yume) while maintaining competitive visual quality. Inference speed is 12 FPS at 540p on a single A100 — generation takes 8 seconds vs. 572s for Yume, 971s for MatrixGame, and 611s for Wan-2.1. Long-video evaluation (30-second T2V) shows Self-Forcing with TSCM maintains stable aesthetic (0.523) and image quality (0.601) scores across 6 video blocks, compared to significant degradation without (0.442 aesthetic, 0.542 image quality at block 6). TSCM maintains constant inference time per step beyond 8 video blocks, unlike full-context methods.

## Limitations
- Resolution tested at 544×960 and 704×1280; scaling to higher resolutions is not evaluated.
- Event generation trained on only 4K curated videos across four categories — coverage of event types is narrow.
- Relies on Wan2.2-5B as the foundation model — performance is bounded by the base model's generative capabilities.
- Keyboard-based control discretizes camera pose space, which limits fine-grained camera trajectory control compared to explicit pose-conditioned methods (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl).
- Evaluation uses VBench-derived metrics, which may not fully capture perceptual quality and interactivity as experienced by human users.

## Key takeaways
- TSCM's dual-path compression (temporal-spatial via standard attention + channel via linear attention) solves the growing-context problem for infinite-horizon autoregressive video generation, achieving constant inference time regardless of video length.
- Self-Forcing with TSCM is more effective than Self-Forcing with KV cache: TSCM acts as a temporal filter that discards error-prone token representations from compressed history, inherently reducing error accumulation.
- Text-controlled event generation can be achieved with minimal data (4K videos) through careful architectural design — decomposing event and action descriptions allows the model to learn event generation without forgetting its base video generation capabilities.
- The 70× speedup over prior interactive world models (8s vs. 572s) demonstrates that diffusion-based world models can achieve real-time interaction through distillation and context compression, not just architectural changes.
