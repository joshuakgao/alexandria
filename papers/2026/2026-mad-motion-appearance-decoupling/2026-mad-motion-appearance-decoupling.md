# MAD: Motion Appearance Decoupling for Efficient Driving World Models

![[2026-mad-motion-appearance-decoupling-thumbnail.png]]

## Research gap
Adapting generalist video diffusion models into driving world models is prohibitively expensive — VISTA and GEM require 25,000–50,000 GPU-hours of fine-tuning, and proprietary models like Cosmos train from scratch on massive datasets. The core difficulty is that driving world models must simultaneously learn two coupled, complex objectives: photorealistic appearance synthesis and physically grounded multi-agent dynamics. No prior work fully separates these responsibilities for efficient adaptation.

## Contributions
- **MAD methodology**: A two-stage "reasoning-rendering" pipeline that decouples motion forecasting from appearance synthesis — a Motion Forecaster generates abstract skeletonized pose videos capturing scene dynamics, then an Appearance Synthesizer renders photorealistic RGB video conditioned on these poses.
- **Efficient adaptation framework**: Reuses a single pretrained video generation backbone with lightweight LoRA adapters for both stages, projecting all conditioning signals (poses, first frame, ego-motion) through the model's native VAE — no new adapter architectures needed.
- **Novel visual ego-motion representation**: Encodes 3D camera pose as a rendered video of a textured sphere with dust particles, where rotations appear as background motion and translations as parallax — a visual encoding the VGM backbone can naturally interpret.
- **MAD-LTX**: An open-source SOTA driving world model (2B and 13B) that outperforms all prior open-source driving models and matches proprietary Cosmos Predict 2 quality, while using a fraction of compute (128–700 GPU-hours vs. 25,000–50,000+).

## Method
**Intermediate motion representation.** The "pose video" renders skeletonized agents (cars via OpenPifPaf, pedestrians via DWPose) and lane lines on a black background with color-coded joints/edges. This is extracted automatically as pseudo-labels from any driving video — no manual annotation or AV perception stack required. Compared to alternatives: HD maps are too abstract and non-scalable; panoptic segmentation lacks 3D orientation; skeleton poses provide the best balance of scalability, 3D-awareness, and visual correlation with the VGM's learned priors.

**Motion Forecaster (Fω).** A LoRA-adapted video diffusion model that generates future pose videos. Conditioned on: (1) clean first-pose-frame latents concatenated with noised future latents, (2) T5-encoded text prompts via cross-attention, (3) VAE-encoded first RGB frame for scene context. Operates at the base model's native resolution/FPS to stay within its "comfort zone." Supports optional ego-motion control (visual sphere representation) and object-motion control (sparse bounding box trajectory videos for up to 5 agents).

**Appearance Synthesizer (Sω).** A second LoRA from the same backbone that renders photorealistic video conditioned on the generated pose video, first RGB frame, and text. Key innovation: **targeted noise injection** during training — Gaussian noise is added only to latent features corresponding to skeleton regions (not background), simulating the artifacts of the Motion Forecaster's predictions. This bridges the train-inference domain gap and improves output sharpness.

**All conditioning through native VAE.** A unifying design principle: every control signal (poses, RGB frame, ego-motion sphere, object trajectories) is encoded through the base model's pretrained VAE and concatenated to the DiT input sequence — no new encoder architectures needed.

## Datasets & evaluation
**Training data:** 100K 5-second clips from OpenDV (139 hours of driving video from YouTube, preprocessed to 24fps at 1056×704). Clips filtered to retain top 50% by object count. Captions generated by Qwen2.5-VL-32B. Waymo Perception dataset used for ablation studies.

**Evaluation:** Primarily human preference studies (pairwise A/B tests across 100 random validation scenes, 14 model comparisons) evaluating general quality, motion realism, and visual quality. Also open-loop motion planning (minADE@6, APD@6 over 5-second horizon using MapAnything trajectory extraction).

**Key results:**
- **MAD-SVD** matches VISTA quality with <6% of its compute (1,500 vs. 25,000 GPU-hours) and <12× less data.
- **MAD-LTX 2B/13B** outperforms all open-source driving models (VISTA, GEM, Cosmos Predict 1) in human preference.
- **MAD-LTX 13B** achieves quality nearly on par with proprietary Cosmos Predict 2 (14B).
- **Planning:** MAD-LTX achieves best minADE@6 (3.64m at 13B) while maintaining trajectory diversity (APD@6 101.45cm), whereas naive fine-tuning degrades diversity by 37.8% and accuracy by 40%.
- **Ablations:** Targeted noise injection preferred 62% over no-noise variant; pose representation preferred 74% over panoptic segmentation and 78% over HD maps.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on human preference studies rather than standardized automated metrics (FID/FVD found to correlate poorly with perceptual quality in driving scenarios).
- Single-view monocular paradigm only — no multi-view or surround-camera support.
- Motion representation limited to visible dynamic agents (cars, pedestrians, lane lines); does not capture all scene elements (e.g., traffic light state changes, construction zones).
- Open-loop evaluation only — no closed-loop driving simulation or downstream policy learning demonstrated.
- Pseudo-label quality from off-the-shelf pose extractors may degrade in adverse conditions (night, rain, occlusion).
- Ego-motion sphere representation is a novel visual encoding whose failure modes under extreme maneuvers are not characterized.

## Key takeaways
- Decoupling motion from appearance mirrors the professional animator's workflow (animatic → rendering) and is dramatically more compute-efficient than joint end-to-end fine-tuning — the same backbone adapted with two lightweight LoRAs matches or exceeds models trained with 16× more compute.
- Operating within the base model's "comfort zone" (native resolution, FPS, VAE-based conditioning) is critical for efficient transfer — downsampling or adding new adapter networks forces the model to learn out-of-distribution priors, vastly increasing data requirements.
- Abstract intermediate representations prevent mode collapse: by removing visual textures, the Motion Forecaster learns underlying dynamics rather than spurious correlations (e.g., always stopping when seeing an ambulance), maintaining trajectory diversity that naive fine-tuning destroys.
- The methodology is backbone-agnostic (demonstrated on SVD and LTX), suggesting it can ride the wave of future VGM improvements with minimal re-adaptation cost.
