# Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer

![[2020-t5-thumbnail.png]]

## Research gap
The rapid proliferation of transfer learning approaches for NLP — spanning different pre-training objectives, architectures, unlabeled datasets, and fine-tuning methods — made it difficult to compare methods and understand which factors actually drive performance. No systematic study had disentangled these factors in a controlled setting.

## Contributions
- Introduces a unified text-to-text framework that casts all NLP tasks (classification, regression, translation, summarization, question answering) into the same input-output format, enabling a single model, loss function, and decoding procedure across tasks.
- Provides a comprehensive empirical survey comparing architectures (encoder-decoder, decoder-only, prefix LM), pre-training objectives (language modeling, denoising, span corruption), unlabeled datasets, transfer approaches (fine-tuning, adapters, multi-task), and scaling strategies.
- Introduces the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus (C4), a cleaned 750GB English text dataset derived from Common Crawl.
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on 18 of 24 tasks at time of publication, including GLUE (90.3), SuperGLUE (88.9, near human-level 89.8), SQuAD, and CNN/Daily Mail, by scaling to 11 billion parameters.
- Releases code, pre-trained models (T5-Small through T5-11B), and the C4 dataset.

## Method
T5 uses the original encoder-decoder Transformer architecture with relative position embeddings, simplified layer normalization (no additive bias), and pre-norm residual connections. All tasks are converted to text-to-text format by prepending a task-specific prefix (e.g., "translate English to German:", "summarize:"). Pre-training uses a span-corruption denoising objective: 15% of input tokens are replaced with sentinel tokens in contiguous spans (mean length 3), and the model learns to reconstruct the corrupted spans. The final model uses multi-task pre-training (mixing unsupervised and supervised tasks with example-proportional mixing) followed by fine-tuning on individual tasks. Training uses 1 trillion tokens on C4 with AdaFactor optimizer. Models range from 60M (Small) to 11B parameters, trained on TPU pods with model and data parallelism via Mesh TensorFlow. Beam search (width 4, length penalty α=0.6) is used for translation and summarization at inference.

## Datasets & evaluation
Pre-training: C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus), ~750GB of heuristically cleaned Common Crawl text. Evaluation spans GLUE (8 tasks), SuperGLUE (8 tasks), SQuAD (extractive QA), CNN/Daily Mail (abstractive summarization), and WMT translation (En-De, En-Fr, En-Ro). T5-11B achieved SOTA on GLUE (90.3), SuperGLUE (88.9, vs. human 89.8), SQuAD (91.26 EM), and CNN/Daily Mail (21.55 ROUGE-2). Translation tasks did not reach SOTA, attributed to English-only pre-training and lack of backtranslation. The systematic study found: encoder-decoder outperforms decoder-only and prefix LM at equivalent compute; denoising objectives outperform language modeling for pre-training; span corruption is slightly better and more efficient than i.i.d. token corruption; larger models trained for fewer steps often outperform smaller models trained longer; and dataset repetition during pre-training is harmful.

## Limitations
- English-only pre-training limits performance on translation tasks and excludes non-English NLP entirely.
- The largest model (11B parameters) is expensive to fine-tune and deploy, limiting practical applicability in resource-constrained settings.
- Denoising pre-training may be an inefficient way to encode world knowledge — the model requires 1 trillion tokens of pre-training to reach peak performance.
- The text-to-text framing adds overhead for tasks (like classification) that don't naturally require generation, since the model must decode full text labels.
- Multi-task mixing proportions remain heuristic; no principled method was found that consistently outperformed simple unsupervised pre-training followed by fine-tuning.

## Key takeaways
- The text-to-text framework is a unifying abstraction: by casting all NLP tasks into the same format, a single model architecture, loss function, and training procedure can match or exceed task-specific approaches. This insight directly enabled subsequent models (mT5, FLAN, UL2) and influenced the "prompt everything" paradigm.
- The encoder-decoder architecture outperforms decoder-only models at equivalent computational cost (not parameter count), despite receiving less attention in subsequent work that favored decoder-only scaling (GPT-3, PaLM).
- Scale is necessary but not sufficient — the systematic study's "non-scaling" insights (span corruption, multi-task pre-training, C4 data quality) provide meaningful gains on top of simply training larger models longer.
- C4 demonstrated that large-scale web data can be effectively cleaned with simple heuristics, establishing a template for subsequent large-scale pre-training corpora (The Pile, RedPajama, etc.).
