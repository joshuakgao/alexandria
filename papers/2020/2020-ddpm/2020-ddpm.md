# Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

![[2020-ddpm-thumbnail.png]]

## Research gap
Diffusion probabilistic models had been proposed theoretically but never demonstrated to generate high-quality samples competitive with GANs, autoregressive models, or flows. It was unclear whether this class of generative model could produce visually compelling results, and the connection between diffusion models and denoising score matching remained implicit.

## Contributions
- Demonstrates that diffusion models can generate high-quality image samples, achieving state-of-the-art FID of 3.17 on unconditional CIFAR-10, surpassing most existing generative models including class-conditional ones.
- Establishes an explicit connection between diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics, showing that training a diffusion model is equivalent to optimizing a weighted denoising score matching objective.
- Introduces the ε-prediction parameterization: instead of predicting the reverse process mean μ̃, the network predicts the noise ε added during the forward process, which simplifies the objective and improves sample quality.
- Proposes a simplified training objective (L_simple) that down-weights loss terms for small noise levels, focusing the network on harder denoising tasks and yielding better samples than the full variational bound.
- Shows that diffusion models admit a progressive lossy decompression scheme that generalizes autoregressive decoding to a continuous bit ordering via Gaussian noise.

## Method
The forward process is a fixed Markov chain that gradually adds Gaussian noise to data over T=1000 steps with a linear variance schedule (β₁=10⁻⁴ to β_T=0.02). The reverse process learns to denoise via a parameterized Markov chain with Gaussian transitions. The model architecture is a U-Net with group normalization, Transformer sinusoidal position embeddings for timestep conditioning, and self-attention at 16×16 resolution. The key insight is the ε-prediction parameterization: rather than predicting the posterior mean μ̃_t, the network predicts the noise ε, yielding μ_θ(x_t, t) = (1/√α_t)(x_t − β_t/√(1−ᾱ_t) · ε_θ(x_t, t)). The simplified objective L_simple = E_{t,x₀,ε}[‖ε − ε_θ(√ᾱ_t x₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)ε, t)‖²] discards the per-timestep weighting from the variational bound, which down-weights small-t terms and improves sample quality. Reverse process variances are fixed (not learned), as learned variances led to unstable training.

## Datasets & evaluation
Evaluated on unconditional CIFAR-10 (32×32), CelebA-HQ (256×256), and LSUN Bedroom/Church (256×256). On CIFAR-10: FID 3.17 (state-of-the-art for unconditional generation), Inception Score 9.46, NLL ≤3.75 bits/dim. LSUN Bedroom FID 4.90, LSUN Church FID 7.89. Sample quality comparable to ProgressiveGAN on 256×256. Log-likelihoods are not competitive with other likelihood-based models (PixelCNN, Sparse Transformer), but the majority of codelength is consumed by imperceptible details — rate-distortion analysis shows the model is an excellent lossy compressor. Ablations confirm ε-prediction with L_simple outperforms μ̃-prediction and training on the full variational bound for sample quality.

## Limitations
- Sampling requires T=1000 sequential neural network evaluations, making generation orders of magnitude slower than GANs or VAEs.
- Log-likelihoods are not competitive with autoregressive models or flows, despite high sample quality — the model allocates most codelength to imperceptible details.
- Fixed reverse process variances are suboptimal; learned variances were explored but caused training instability, leaving potential performance on the table.
- Only demonstrated on unconditional generation — no class-conditional or text-conditional generation was explored.
- The linear noise schedule is a heuristic; no principled method for selecting the schedule is provided.

## Key takeaways
- The ε-prediction parameterization is the critical design choice: it connects diffusion models to denoising score matching, simplifies the training objective, and enables high sample quality. This parameterization became the default in essentially all subsequent diffusion model work (DDIM, Improved DDPM, Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, Imagen).
- The simplified objective L_simple demonstrates that better samples can be obtained by deviating from the true variational bound — down-weighting easy (low-noise) denoising tasks focuses the model on perceptually important structure. This insight recurs in subsequent work on loss weighting and noise schedule design.
- Diffusion models' progressive generation (coarse-to-fine, large features first) provides a natural hierarchical decomposition that autoregressive models cannot easily achieve, and connects generation to lossy compression.
- The gap between sample quality and log-likelihood reveals a fundamental tension in generative modeling: optimizing for perceptual quality (FID) and optimizing for exact density estimation (NLL) may require different trade-offs, with diffusion models favoring the former.
