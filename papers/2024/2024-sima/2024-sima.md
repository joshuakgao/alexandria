# Scaling Instructable Agents Across Many Simulated Worlds

![[2024-sima-thumbnail.png]]

## Research gap
Prior work on embodied agents in 3D environments typically focused on single games or narrow research environments with simplified action spaces and privileged state access. No existing system demonstrated language-instructable agents that generalize across many visually diverse, mechanically complex commercial video games using only a human-like interface (pixels in, keyboard-and-mouse out).

## Contributions
- Introduces SIMA, an agent trained to follow free-form natural language instructions across 10+ diverse 3D environments including commercial video games and research environments.
- Uses a generic, human-like interface (image observations and language in, keyboard-and-mouse actions out) with no access to game state, rewards, or custom APIs.
- Demonstrates positive transfer: a single multi-environment agent outperforms environment-specialized agents by 67% on average.
- Shows zero-shot transfer of basic skills to entirely unseen environments.
- Applies classifier-free guidance (CFG) to improve language conditionality of behavioral cloning agents.
- Develops a comprehensive evaluation framework combining ground-truth, OCR-based, and human evaluation methods across 1,485 unique tasks.

## Method
The SIMA agent maps image observations and language instructions to keyboard-and-mouse actions. The architecture combines pretrained vision encoders (SPARC for fine-grained image-text alignment, Phenaki for video prediction) with trained-from-scratch transformers that cross-attend to vision features, encoded language, and a Transformer-XL memory module. A policy network outputs sequences of 8 keyboard-and-mouse actions. Training uses behavioral cloning on human gameplay data, with an auxiliary goal-completion prediction objective. At inference, classifier-free guidance shifts policy logits toward the language-conditioned direction: π_CFG = π(image, language) + λ(π(image, language) − π(image, ·)). The agent focuses on short-horizon tasks (~10 seconds), with longer tasks decomposed into sub-instructions.

## Datasets & evaluation
Data was collected from human experts across 10+ environments (7 commercial video games including Goat Simulator 3, Hydroneer, No Man's Sky, Satisfactory, Teardown, Valheim, Wobbly Life; and research environments Construction Lab, Playhouse, WorldLab, ProcTHOR). Collections include single-player gameplay with post-hoc annotation and two-player setter-solver interactions. Evaluation uses ground-truth task completion in research environments, OCR-based detection in games with on-screen text (No Man's Sky, Valheim), and expert human judges reviewing recorded agent videos (5 ratings per episode). The evaluation set spans 1,485 unique tasks across 9 skill categories. On a No Man's Sky subset, human experts achieved only 60% success, while SIMA achieved 34% (vs. 11% for the no-language ablation). SIMA significantly outperformed environment-specialized agents across all 7 evaluated environments (permutation test p < 0.05 in every case).

## Limitations
- Agent performance remains well below human level, particularly on complex tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning, tool use, or construction.
- Focus is limited to short-horizon tasks (~10 seconds); long-horizon planning and multi-step task composition are not addressed.
- Commercial video game evaluations are expensive, slow, and rely heavily on subjective human judgments or narrow OCR-based metrics.
- Pretrained vision encoders were fine-tuned on a subset of environments, partially confounding zero-shot transfer results (except for Goat Simulator 3).
- The approach relies entirely on behavioral cloning from human demonstrations, with no online learning or reinforcement signal.

## Key takeaways
- Training across diverse environments yields strong positive transfer—multi-environment agents consistently outperform single-environment specialists—supporting the hypothesis that breadth of experience is key to general embodied AI.
- A human-like interface (pixels + keyboard/mouse) trades off sample efficiency for generality: the same agent architecture works across all environments without per-game customization.
- Classifier-free guidance, borrowed from diffusion models, substantially improves language conditionality in behavioral cloning agents.
- Zero-shot transfer of basic skills (navigation, color-based object interaction) to unseen games suggests the emergence of transferable embodied representations.
- Commercial video games provide a scalable, diverse, and challenging testbed for embodied AI research, bridging the gap between narrow research environments and real-world robotics.
