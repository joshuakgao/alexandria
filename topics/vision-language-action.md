## Overview
Vision-language-action (VLA) models extend vision-language models by adding action prediction capabilities for robot control. These models leverage internet-scale pretraining on images and text, then fine-tune on robot demonstration data to output low-level motor commands. VLAs represent a convergence of foundation model scaling, imitation learning, and embodied AI, aiming to create generalist robot policies that can interpret natural language instructions and act in the physical world. RT-2 (Google DeepMind, CoRL 2023) coined the term and established the paradigm by showing that VLMs can be co-fine-tuned on web and robot data to directly output tokenized actions, inheriting emergent semantic reasoning from web pretraining. DexVLA (CoRL 2025) challenged the assumption that scaling should focus on the VLM backbone, demonstrating that a billion-parameter diffusion action expert paired with a small VLM achieves strong cross-embodiment performance from only 100 hours of data. GR00T N1 (NVIDIA, 2025) introduced the data pyramid strategy for humanoid robots — layering web/human videos, synthetic data, and real demonstrations — with a dual-system architecture (VLM reasoning at 10Hz + DiT action generation at 120Hz), achieving 76.8% success on real-world GR-1 humanoid tasks and strong data efficiency. ChatVLA-2 (2025) addresses the fundamental tension between robot fine-tuning and VLM knowledge preservation, introducing dynamic MoE to disentangle reasoning from control and a two-stage training strategy that enables open-world embodied reasoning — achieving 82.7% success on unseen math equations where all prior VLAs score near zero. JARVIS-VLA (Team CraftJarvis, ACL 2025 Findings) extends the VLA paradigm to open-world game environments, introducing ActVLP — a multi-stage post-training pipeline that enhances the VLM with environment-specific world knowledge, visual recognition, and spatial grounding *before* action fine-tuning. This staged approach yields 40% improvement over baselines in Minecraft across 1,000+ atomic tasks, demonstrating that investing in the foundation model's environment understanding pays larger dividends than scaling trajectory data alone. OmniJARVIS (Team CraftJarvis, NeurIPS 2024) tackles the fundamental tension between reasoning and efficient control by introducing unified VLA tokenization: a self-supervised FSQ-based behavior tokenizer compresses 128-step trajectories into 5 discrete tokens, which are augmented into a pretrained MLM's vocabulary alongside vision and language tokens. By modeling multimodal interaction sequences (instruction, memory, chain-of-thought, observation, behavior tokens) autoregressively, OmniJARVIS reasons, plans, and acts within a single model — achieving 0.59 average success on long-horizon programmatic Minecraft tasks vs. 0.43 for the best pipeline baseline (DEPS), while also handling open-ended QA and instruction following.

## Trends
- **Actions as language tokens**: RT-2 established the foundational approach of discretizing robot actions into text tokens and training VLMs to predict them alongside natural language, requiring no architectural modifications.
- **Co-fine-tuning over naive fine-tuning**: Jointly training on web-scale vision-language data and robot trajectories is critical for generalization — robot-only fine-tuning degrades the semantic reasoning inherited from pretraining.
- **Emergent capabilities from web pretraining**: VLA models exhibit reasoning capabilities never seen in robot training data — symbol understanding, inter-object relation reasoning, and chain-of-thought inference — transferred from internet-scale pretraining.
- **Scaling action experts independently from VLM backbones**: DexVLA demonstrates that scaling the diffusion-based action expert to 1B parameters while keeping the VLM small (2B) is a viable alternative to scaling the language model.
- **Curriculum learning strategies** that progressively train on harder tasks across multiple embodiments.
- **Integration of diffusion models** as action decoders for handling multimodal action distributions.
- **Sub-step reasoning annotations** to enable end-to-end long-horizon task completion without external high-level planners.
- **Data pyramid for humanoid robots**: GR00T N1 structures heterogeneous training data by scale — web videos and human demonstrations at the base, synthetic/neural trajectories in the middle, real robot data at the top — with latent-action codebooks and IDM pseudo-labels to learn from action-less video sources.
- **Dual-system architectures**: Separating slow reasoning (VLM at 10Hz) from fast action generation (DiT at 120Hz) via cross-attention, enabling real-time humanoid control while retaining language understanding.
- **Neural trajectory augmentation**: Fine-tuning video generation models on robot data to produce counterfactual training videos with pseudo-action labels, consistently improving performance across data regimes.
- **Mixture-of-Experts for knowledge preservation**: ChatVLA introduced static MoE on MLP layers (control expert + understanding expert with shared attention) to isolate task-specific representations while enabling cross-task knowledge transfer. ChatVLA-2 extended this to dynamic MoE (8 experts, top-2 routing) to further disentangle multi-modal understanding from robot control within the VLM backbone.
- **Spurious forgetting over catastrophic forgetting**: ChatVLA's systematic analysis reveals that VLA fine-tuning causes "spurious forgetting" — not true knowledge erasure but misalignment of visual-text representations. The finding that even structured reasoning templates can partially reactivate conversational ability suggests VLM knowledge persists but becomes inaccessible through standard prompting after robot training.
- **Phased alignment training**: ChatVLA established the curriculum-based approach of first training on robot data (to master control), then co-training with visual-text data (to reactivate understanding). ChatVLA-2 reversed and extended this: co-training first (to establish reasoning), then freezing the VLM and training only the action expert (to strengthen reasoning-following), enabling VLAs to generalize to entirely unseen reasoning scenarios.
- **Reasoning-following action experts**: Injecting VLM reasoning tokens into the action expert's conditioning (via scale/shift parameters in latter-half layers) creates a tighter coupling between open-world reasoning and motor execution than FiLM-based approaches.
- **Environment-specific VL post-training before action learning**: JARVIS-VLA's ActVLP paradigm demonstrates that a structured 3-stage pipeline — text-only world knowledge → multimodal visual/spatial grounding → action imitation learning — substantially outperforms both direct imitation learning and single-stage co-training. Spatial grounding contributes the most among non-trajectory tasks, and the approach achieves superior results with only 21% of the trajectory data used by IL-only baselines.
- **VLAs beyond robotics**: JARVIS-VLA extends the VLA paradigm from physical robot manipulation to open-world game environments with keyboard-and-mouse control, repurposing least-used vocabulary tokens for action representation. This parallels SIMA/SIMA 2's human-interface approach and suggests VLA architectures are domain-agnostic.
- **Behavior tokenization as a third action representation**: OmniJARVIS introduces self-supervised FSQ-based behavior tokens as an alternative to both raw action tokens (RT-2, JARVIS-VLA) and language goals (Voyager, DEPS). By compressing 128-step trajectories into 5 discrete tokens via a learned encoder-decoder, behavior tokens offer a compact yet semantically rich intermediate between text goals (which lose context-dependent nuance) and per-step action prediction (which requires impractical sequence lengths for long-horizon tasks). The approach enables a single autoregressive model to reason via chain-of-thought *and* act via behavior token emission, unifying the planner-controller split.
- **Interaction data as training signal**: OmniJARVIS demonstrates that modeling the full decision-making interaction — instruction, memory, chain-of-thought reasoning, observation, behavior — in a single autoregressive sequence yields better behavior prediction than modeling trajectories alone. LLM-synthesized thoughts are particularly impactful, confirming that intermediate reasoning representations bridge the gap between observations and actions even when generated post-hoc rather than captured from humans.

- **Unsupervised pretraining via latent actions**: LAPA (Ye et al., ICLR 2025) demonstrates that VLA models can be pretrained without any ground-truth action labels by learning discrete latent actions via VQ-VAE from consecutive video frames, then training a VLM to predict these latent actions. This outperforms OpenVLA (trained with ground-truth actions) by +6.2% on real-world manipulation with 30x greater pretraining efficiency, and shows positive transfer even from human manipulation videos alone — opening the path to web-scale robotics foundation models without teleoperation.
- **Latent action spaces as embodiment-agnostic representations**: LAPA's latent actions provide a universal intermediate between raw pixels and embodiment-specific motor commands. By avoiding ground-truth action labels during pretraining, the model avoids overfitting to source action spaces (e.g., WidowX), enabling better cross-embodiment transfer than action-labeled pretraining.
- **Explicit 3D hand actions from in-the-wild human video**: VITRA (Li et al., ICRA 2026) shows that unstructured egocentric human videos can be fully automatically converted into VLA-aligned training data with explicit 3D action labels — recovering metric-scale hand poses via monocular reconstruction, segmenting atomic actions via wrist speed minima, and generating instructions via GPT-4.1. The resulting 1M-episode dataset vastly exceeds robot datasets in visual and linguistic diversity, and explicit 3D labels outperform latent actions (LAPA-style) for dexterous manipulation pretraining (71.0% vs. 46.0% on seen tasks).
- **Data diversity over data scale for pretraining**: VITRA demonstrates that visual and linguistic diversity of pretraining data matters more than sheer episode count — in-the-wild data outperforms lab-captured EgoDex (300K+ episodes) even at smaller subset sizes, with clear log-linear scaling behavior as data grows.

## Open questions
- How to achieve true zero-shot transfer across entirely new robot morphologies without embodiment-specific heads.
- Optimal balance between VLM backbone scale and action expert scale for data-efficient learning — RT-2 scales VLMs to 55B while DexVLA scales action experts to 1B with a 2B VLM.
- Reducing reliance on human demonstrations through simulation, self-play, or other automated data generation.
- Handling contact-rich, deformable-object manipulation reliably in long-horizon settings.
- Whether latent action pretraining scales to true web-scale video — LAPA used curated datasets (BridgeV2, Open-X, Something-Something v2), not raw internet video, and grasping performance still lags behind coarse planning due to rare grasp events in training data. VITRA's explicit 3D labels significantly outperform latent actions on dexterous tasks, but require accurate monocular 3D hand reconstruction — the optimal action representation for human-video pretraining (latent vs. explicit 3D) may depend on the target embodiment.
- Whether the human-to-robot action space gap for dexterous hands can be closed by simple topological joint mapping (as in VITRA) or requires more sophisticated retargeting — VITRA's simple mapping works for a 12-DoF hand, but higher-DOF hands may need learned mappings.
- Whether the actions-as-tokens approach (RT-2) or dedicated diffusion action experts (DexVLA, GR00T N1) is more effective as both data and model scale increase.
- Catastrophic forgetting during post-training — GR00T N1 shows that task-specific fine-tuning can erase pre-trained capabilities (e.g., inter-hand transfer lost after right-hand-only data).
- Quality and physical fidelity of neural trajectory generation — current video models still struggle with counterfactual diversity and physics adherence.
- How to fully preserve VLM pre-trained knowledge during robot fine-tuning — ChatVLA's static MoE and ChatVLA-2's dynamic MoE mitigate but do not eliminate capability loss. The optimal MoE configuration (static vs. dynamic, number of experts, routing strategy) for different VLM architectures is unexplored.
- Whether spurious forgetting (ChatVLA) is a universal phenomenon across VLM architectures or specific to certain backbone families — and whether the degree of recoverability varies with model scale or pretraining data composition.
- Whether open-world reasoning generalizes beyond tabletop manipulation to mobile manipulation and long-horizon tasks requiring sustained reasoning chains.
- Whether ActVLP's staged post-training approach transfers to new domains beyond Minecraft — constructing equivalent non-trajectory VL datasets for each environment is costly, and it is unclear whether the spatial grounding → action learning pipeline generalizes without domain-specific data curation.
- How to extend VLA models from atomic task execution to long-horizon compositional tasks (e.g., ObtainDiamond in Minecraft requires ~24,000 actions) — JARVIS-VLA evaluates only atomic tasks, and whether ActVLP's environment understanding benefits compound over multi-step plans is untested.
- Optimal granularity for behavior tokenization — OmniJARVIS uses fixed 128-step trunks with 5 FSQ tokens, but whether adaptive chunking (variable-length behaviors) or hierarchical tokenization (sub-behavior composition) would improve long-horizon planning is unexplored. The 0.08 diamond-tier success rate suggests current behavior token granularity is still too coarse for deeply compositional tasks.
