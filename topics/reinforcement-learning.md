## Overview

*Last updated: 2026-07-08 | Sources: 14 papers*

This topic covers reinforcement learning methodology, infrastructure, and training paradigms. The Dreamer lineage (V1→V2→V3) represents the most complete arc: DQN (2013) launched deep RL from pixels; PPO (2017) became the standard on-policy algorithm; RND (Burda et al., ICLR 2019) introduced prediction error against fixed random networks as a scalable exploration bonus, achieving superhuman Montezuma's Revenge without demonstrations; World Models (2018) established dream training; Dreamer (ICLR 2020) added differentiable actor-critic imagination; DreamerV2 (ICLR 2021) achieved human-level Atari via categorical latents; and DreamerV3 (Nature 2025) achieved domain-general RL — outperforming specialized experts across 150+ tasks with fixed hyperparameters and collecting diamonds in Minecraft from scratch. VPT (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022) demonstrates the three-phase paradigm (pretraining → BC fine-tuning → RL fine-tuning): a behavioral prior trained on ~70K hours of IDM-pseudo-labeled internet Minecraft video enables RL fine-tuning to achieve diamond pickaxe crafting — a task impossible for RL from scratch in the native human interface. Genie (ICML 2024) removes action supervision via unsupervised latent action discovery. Gymnasium (NeurIPS 2025) provides the foundational environment API. SIMA (Google DeepMind, 2024) demonstrates that behavioral cloning across 10+ diverse 3D environments with pretrained vision encoders and classifier-free guidance can outperform environment-specialized agents, offering an alternative to RL for multi-environment embodied control.

RL methods also appear as components in other papers in this wiki: ViewSuite uses PPO and GRPO for self-exploration in view planning, and NitroGen uses behavior cloning (a supervised alternative to RL) trained on Gymnasium-compatible environments.

## Trends

- **Deep Q-learning from pixels**: DQN (Mnih et al., 2013) demonstrated that combining convolutional networks with Q-learning and experience replay enables learning control policies directly from raw pixels — a single architecture with no game-specific tuning outperforms all prior RL methods on 6/7 Atari games and surpasses human experts on 3. Experience replay breaks temporal correlations and stabilizes the non-stationary data distribution, transforming RL into something closer to i.i.d. supervised learning. This established deep RL as a general-purpose method and directly inspired the subsequent explosion of deep RL research (A3C, PPO, AlphaGo).
- **Clipped surrogate objectives for stable policy optimization**: PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) replaces TRPO's second-order constrained optimization with a simple clipped probability ratio objective — clip(r_t, 1-ε, 1+ε) — that prevents destructively large policy updates while allowing multiple epochs of minibatch SGD on the same trajectory data. This achieves TRPO's stability with first-order simplicity, making PPO the default on-policy algorithm in practice (used in RLHF for ChatGPT, robotics locomotion, and as a component in ViewSuite's exploration training). The key design principle — pessimistic lower bounds via min of clipped/unclipped objectives — is broadly applicable beyond RL.
- **Prediction error as scalable exploration bonus**: RND (Burda et al., ICLR 2019) introduces an exploration bonus that is the MSE between a trained predictor network and a fixed randomly initialized target network applied to the current observation. Because the target is deterministic and within the predictor's model class, this eliminates both aleatoric uncertainty (the "noisy TV" problem that plagues forward dynamics bonuses) and model misspecification as sources of prediction error — leaving only epistemic uncertainty (genuinely novel states). Combined with non-episodic intrinsic returns (not truncated at game over) and dual value heads for separate extrinsic/intrinsic reward streams, RND achieves mean return of 8,152 on Montezuma's Revenge — the first method to exceed average human performance without demonstrations or emulator access. The connection to Bayesian uncertainty quantification (distillation error approximates predictive variance of an ensemble) provides theoretical grounding for this empirically simple method.
- **Model-based RL via dream training**: World Models (2018) demonstrates that agents can learn entirely inside hallucinated environments generated by learned dynamics models, transferring policies to reality. The key insight — stochastic world models (MDN-RNN with adjustable temperature) prevent adversarial exploitation by the controller — remains relevant as dream training scales to more complex domains.
- **Analytic gradient propagation through learned dynamics**: Dreamer (Hafner et al., ICLR 2020) advances dream training from derivative-free optimization (CEM in PlaNet, CMA-ES in World Models) to differentiable actor-critic learning in latent space. By backpropagating analytic gradients of λ-return value estimates through imagined trajectories, Dreamer combines the data efficiency of model-based methods with the asymptotic performance of model-free methods — achieving average return of 823 on 20 DM Control tasks at 5M steps vs. D4PG's 786 at 100M steps (20× more data). The value model is critical: it enables long-horizon credit assignment beyond any fixed imagination horizon, solving tasks (acrobot, hopper) where horizon-only methods fail completely.
- **Categorical latents close the model-based/model-free gap**: DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., ICLR 2021) replaces Gaussian latents with 32×32 categorical variables and introduces KL balancing (asymmetric prior/posterior learning rates), becoming the first world model agent to achieve human-level Atari performance — outperforming IQN and Rainbow on the Atari-55 benchmark at 200M frames on a single GPU. The 10,000× imagination amplification (468B imagined states from 50M real interactions) is the mechanism. Notably, reward gradients are unnecessary for representation learning — general environment representations outperform reward-specific ones.
- **Scale-invariant objectives enable domain-general RL**: DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., Nature 2025) eliminates per-domain tuning via symlog predictions (compressing large targets), symexp twohot distributions (decoupling gradient magnitude from target magnitude), percentile-based return normalization (preserving reward frequency information), and free bits (preventing KL losses from dominating). With fixed hyperparameters, DreamerV3 outperforms specialized experts across 150+ tasks in 8 domains — including Atari (beats MuZero), DMLab (1000% data-efficiency gain over IMPALA), and Minecraft (first algorithm to collect diamonds from scratch without human data). Predictable scaling with model size suggests world model RL may follow scaling laws analogous to language modeling.
- **Unsupervised latent actions as RL interface**: Genie (Bruce et al., ICML 2024) discovers discrete latent actions from unlabelled video via VQ-VAE, then uses them for behavioral cloning — a frozen LAM labels expert videos with latent actions, a policy is trained to predict these, and a small set of expert ground-truth actions maps latent to real actions. This achieves oracle-matching performance on CoinRun with only 200 action-mapping samples, demonstrating that unsupervised video observation can produce the action representations that RL policies require.
- **Internet-scale pretraining as RL exploration prior**: VPT (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022) demonstrates that behavioral cloning on ~70K hours of IDM-pseudo-labeled internet video produces a foundation model whose zero-shot capabilities (tree chopping, crafting tables — ~970 consecutive actions) serve as an effective exploration prior for RL fine-tuning. The three-phase paradigm (pretraining → BC fine-tuning → RL fine-tuning) achieves diamond pickaxe crafting (2.5% success, first reported result with native human interface) — a task where RL from scratch fails to reliably collect even logs. KL divergence to the frozen pretrained policy during RL fine-tuning is critical: without it, catastrophic forgetting of downstream skills (furnace smelting, iron crafting) stalls progress. The inverse dynamics model at the core of VPT is two orders of magnitude more data-efficient than direct behavioral cloning, because inverting environment mechanics is far simpler than modeling the full distribution of human behavior — a principle that connects to the Dreamer insight that world models can be learned from much less data than policies.
- **Self-play fails for human-AI cooperation**: Carroll et al. (NeurIPS 2019) demonstrated that agents trained via self-play or population-based training perform dramatically worse when paired with humans than with copies of themselves. Training with even a crude behavior-cloned human model significantly improves coordination, establishing that cooperative settings require fundamentally different training approaches than competitive ones. FCP (Strouse et al., NeurIPS 2021) resolves this without human data: by best-responding to a diverse pool of self-play agents and their checkpoints, FCP agents outperform even human-data-trained BCP agents in both task performance and human subjective preference.
- **Communicative world models for multi-agent RL**: CoDreamer (Toledo & Prorok, RLC 2024) extends DreamerV3 to multi-agent settings with GNN-based communication within the RSSM and actor-critic networks. Independent world models cannot capture inter-agent dependent dynamics; communication enables accurate imagined trajectories and significantly outperforms both IDreamer and IPPO, especially in pixel-based cooperative tasks.
- **Evolution strategies for compact controllers**: World Models uses CMA-ES rather than gradient-based RL (e.g., policy gradient, A3C) to optimize a minimal linear controller, sidestepping the credit assignment problem by keeping the policy small and offloading representational capacity to the world model. This modular approach (large learned model + small optimized policy) anticipates modern foundation model + RL fine-tuning paradigms.
- **Infrastructure matters**: Gymnasium demonstrates that API design choices (termination vs. truncation, functional vs. object-oriented) directly impact training correctness and research reproducibility across the entire RL ecosystem.
- **RL as component, not end goal**: In the wiki's papers, RL increasingly appears as one stage in a larger pipeline — e.g., ViewSuite's PPO for exploration followed by view graph distillation, or NitroGen's behavior cloning pre-training followed by RL fine-tuning.
- **Sparse reward remains a bottleneck**: ViewSuite shows that direct RL (PPO, GRPO) fails to bootstrap from near-zero reward (~2.5% success), requiring alternative supervision (view graph distillation). This is a general challenge for RL in complex reasoning tasks.
- **Kinesthetic reward shaping for physics-rich games**: Lucy-SKG (Moschopoulos et al., 2023) introduces Kinesthetic Reward Combination (KRC), replacing linear reward component weighting with parameterized functions that capture multiplicative interactions between physical quantities (e.g., ball-goal alignment weighted by velocity and proximity). Combined with on-policy auxiliary tasks (reward prediction, state representation) and a simplified observation space with action stacking, Lucy-SKG achieves state-of-the-art Rocket League play (300-4 vs Necto) with significantly improved sample efficiency — demonstrating that careful reward engineering remains a powerful lever even for PPO-based agents in complex 3D environments.
- **PPO as surprisingly strong cooperative MARL baseline**: Yu et al. (NeurIPS 2022) demonstrate that MAPPO (PPO + centralized value function) matches or exceeds off-policy SOTA (QMix, RODE, MADDPG) across four cooperative benchmarks — MPE, SMAC (23 maps), Google Research Football (6 scenarios), and Hanabi (2–5 players) — with comparable sample efficiency. The critical insight: multi-agent non-stationarity requires more conservative PPO than single-agent settings — fewer epochs (5–10 vs. 30+), no mini-batch splitting, and small clipping ε (<0.2). Centralized value functions become essential as agent count grows, with the MAPPO-IPPO gap widening significantly in 5-player Hanabi. This overturned the prevailing belief that off-policy methods dominate multi-agent RL and established MAPPO as the community-standard cooperative MARL baseline.
- **CTDE as dominant multi-agent training paradigm**: Li et al.'s comprehensive MARL survey (IEEE ToG, 2025) identifies Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) as the prevailing paradigm across all major game genres — from StarCraft II and Dota 2 to Rocket League and Google Research Football. This reflects the practical requirement that agents need shared information during training for coordination but must act independently at inference time. The survey's five-dimensional game complexity framework (Observability, State Space, Action Space, Reward Sparsity, Multi-Agent Scale) provides a systematic way to compare RL challenges across environments.
- **Behavioral cloning with pretrained encoders as RL alternative**: SIMA (Google DeepMind, 2024) forgoes RL entirely, using behavioral cloning on human gameplay data across 10+ environments combined with pretrained vision encoders (SPARC, Phenaki) and classifier-free guidance at inference. The multi-environment agent outperforms per-environment specialists by 67% on average, suggesting that internet-scale pretraining + diverse behavioral cloning data may substitute for RL reward signals in embodied settings — though performance remains well below human level.
- **RL fine-tuning of foundation-model agents**: SIMA 2 (Google DeepMind, 2025) demonstrates that online RL from verifiable rewards applied to a Gemini-based VLA agent — after SFT on human gameplay and synthetic bridge data — doubles embodied task success rates over the BC-only SIMA 1. The RL phase uses curated verifiable tasks (initial state + instruction + verification function) from training environments, with shaped rewards for instruction following and controllability. This validates the pretraining → SFT → RL pipeline for foundation-model embodied agents, paralleling VPT's three-phase paradigm but operating on a natively multimodal foundation model rather than a vision-only behavioral prior.
- **Theory-of-Mind in world model imagination for cooperative RL**: Dreaming of Others (Leroy-Stone, WMW 2026) proposes augmenting Dreamer-style imagination with factorized teammate latents and a ToM head, enabling RL policy optimization over imagined rollouts that include simulated partner behavior. This extends the dream training paradigm from single-agent to cooperative MARL without requiring centralized training or shared imagination — a conceptual proposal awaiting empirical validation.
- **Meta-learned agent modeling as alternative to hand-crafted inference**: Machine Theory of Mind (Rabinowitz et al., ICML 2018) demonstrated that a neural network observer (ToMnet) can learn to model RL agents via meta-learning — acquiring a general prior over agent behavior and bootstrapping agent-specific predictions from few behavioral observations. The character/mental-state factorization anticipates later work on teammate modeling (Dreaming of Others) and shows that classic cognitive tasks (false belief recognition) emerge naturally from the meta-learning objective without explicit programming.
- **Test-time adaptation of world models during MPC planning**: AdaJEPA (Wang et al., 2026) introduces a plan–execute–adapt–replan loop where the JEPA world model is updated with a single self-supervised gradient step after each MPC action execution — using the observed transition as a prediction target. This yields substantial planning improvements under visual, dynamics, shape, and layout distribution shifts, with negligible latency overhead. The key insight is that the MPC loop itself generates the adaptation signal: actions produce observations, observations provide prediction targets, and the updated model improves subsequent planning. This represents an alternative to training-time robustness (PROWL, data augmentation) — rather than building a more robust frozen model, adapt continuously at deployment.
- **RL as adversarial curriculum for world model improvement**: PROWL (Güzel et al., 2026) inverts the typical RL-world-model relationship: instead of training a policy inside a world model (Dreamer), a PPO-trained adversarial policy actively discovers trajectories that expose world model failures. The policy maximizes the world model's prediction error as reward, KL-regularized against a frozen VPT behavioral reference to prevent reward hacking (camera-thrashing). The world model is then fine-tuned on these adversarially discovered trajectories via a Prioritized Adversarial Trajectory buffer. This extends the adversarial curriculum paradigm (UED, PLR) from environment/level generation to world model training — the prioritized buffer trains the simulator, not the agent inhabiting it. The KL anchoring result is particularly relevant: weakly constrained adversaries (c_kl=0.5) degenerate into degenerate action patterns that inflate error without improving the model, echoing VPT's finding that KL to a frozen prior is critical for productive RL fine-tuning.

## Open questions

- Whether model-free deep RL (DQN lineage) or model-based approaches (World Models lineage) will prove more scalable for complex real-world tasks — DQN learns directly from interaction but is sample-inefficient; world models enable dream training but require accurate dynamics. PPO occupies a middle ground as the workhorse on-policy algorithm, but remains less sample-efficient than off-policy methods
- Whether prediction-error exploration bonuses (RND) generalize beyond Atari to environments with high-dimensional continuous observations where many irrelevant dimensions change frequently — and whether the "dancing with skulls" failure mode (agent gravitates toward dangerous but novel-looking states rather than task-relevant ones) can be addressed without task-specific shaping
- Whether on-policy methods (PPO) or off-policy methods (DQN, SAC) will dominate as environments become more expensive to simulate — PPO's requirement for fresh trajectory data each iteration is a fundamental sample-efficiency limitation
- Whether hardware-accelerated functional environments (JAX/GPU) will fundamentally change RL scaling laws by removing the environment simulation bottleneck
- How to bridge the gap between standardized discrete/continuous action spaces (Gymnasium) and the richer action semantics needed for embodied agents (language-conditioned, multi-modal)
- Whether behavior cloning at internet scale (NitroGen, SIMA) can eventually match or surpass RL for embodied policy learning — SIMA 2's results suggest that BC alone is insufficient and RL fine-tuning provides a step-change improvement, but SIMA 2's RL is limited to training environments and doesn't yet transfer to held-out settings
- Whether unsupervised latent action spaces (Genie) can scale to continuous or high-dimensional action spaces needed for complex manipulation and locomotion
- Whether multiplicative reward shaping (KRC) generalizes beyond Rocket League to other physics-rich team games (FIFA, NBA) and whether automated reward design can replace manual tuning
- Whether adversarial curricula for world model training (PROWL) scale beyond MineRL to environments with continuous action spaces, higher-dimensional observations, and more expensive simulation — and whether the reward hacking failure mode (camera-thrashing under weak KL constraints) manifests differently in other domains
- Whether test-time world model adaptation (AdaJEPA) and training-time robustness (PROWL, data augmentation) are complementary — and whether adaptation can safely accumulate across episodes or must reset each time to avoid drift
