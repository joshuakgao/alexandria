## Overview

Image & Video Generation encompasses papers on generative models for visual content — from foundational denoising diffusion probabilistic models and unconditional image synthesis to text-conditioned generation, video synthesis, inpainting, and interactive environments. DDPM (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) established diffusion models as competitive with GANs for image generation via the epsilon-prediction parameterization and a simplified training objective. Video Diffusion Models (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2022) extended diffusion to video with a factorized space-time 3D U-Net, joint image-video training, and reconstruction-guided sampling. Stable Video Diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023) scaled video generation to practical quality through systematic data curation of ~600M clips and a three-stage training pipeline, demonstrating that video models also serve as strong implicit 3D priors for multi-view synthesis. VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al., 2017) introduced discrete latent representations via vector quantization, enabling both high-fidelity reconstruction and downstream generation. CogVideo (Hong et al., ICLR 2023) scaled autoregressive transformers to text-to-video generation by inheriting from a pretrained text-to-image model (CogView2) via dual-channel attention and multi-frame-rate hierarchical training. GAIA-1 (Hu et al., 2023) combines autoregressive token prediction with video diffusion decoding for controllable driving video generation conditioned on text, action, and video inputs. Genie (Bruce et al., ICML 2024) extended the scope to interactive environments, generating frame-by-frame controllable worlds from image prompts using autoregressive MaskGIT-based dynamics models conditioned on learned latent actions.

## Trends

- **Diffusion as the dominant generative paradigm**: DDPM demonstrated that iterative denoising with a simple MSE loss on predicted noise can match or exceed GANs on sample quality metrics, without adversarial training instabilities. The epsilon-prediction parameterization became universal in subsequent work (DDIM, Improved DDPM, Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, Imagen).
- **Sample quality vs. log-likelihood trade-off**: DDPM's simplified objective intentionally deviates from the true variational bound, down-weighting easy denoising tasks to focus on perceptually important structure. This revealed that optimizing for sample quality (FID) and density estimation (NLL) require different trade-offs.
- **Knowledge transfer from image to video generation**: CogVideo demonstrated that inheriting from pretrained text-to-image models (via dual-channel attention with frozen base parameters) is far more efficient than training video models from scratch. This established the paradigm of image-to-video transfer that later influenced SVD's multi-stage training pipeline and GAIA-1's factored architecture.
- **Frame rate as a semantic variable**: CogVideo's multi-frame-rate hierarchical training revealed that matching frame rate to action duration is critical for text-video alignment — a low frame rate captures complete actions (e.g., "drinking water") while recursive interpolation recovers temporal smoothness. This insight influenced subsequent hierarchical video generation approaches.
- **From images to video via minimal architectural changes**: Video Diffusion Models showed that factorized space-time attention in a 3D U-Net, combined with joint image-video training and reconstruction-guided sampling, suffices to extend image diffusion to temporally coherent video. SVD scaled this by demonstrating that data curation and multi-stage training matter more than architecture.
- **Data curation as the dominant lever for video quality**: SVD demonstrated that systematic data curation (cut detection, optical flow filtering, aesthetic scoring) during pretraining produces quality gains that persist through high-quality finetuning.
- **Video models as implicit 3D priors**: SVD showed that finetuning a video diffusion model for multi-view synthesis outperforms specialized 3D methods at a fraction of compute, suggesting video generation and 3D understanding are deeply linked.
- **Hybrid autoregressive + diffusion architectures**: GAIA-1 factored video generation into autoregressive next-token prediction (for dynamics reasoning) and video diffusion (for pixel rendering), enabling each component to scale independently. This two-stage approach became influential for subsequent video world models.
- **From static to interactive generation**: Genie moves beyond generating fixed images or video clips to producing frame-by-frame interactive environments controllable via latent actions discovered from unlabelled video.
- **Diffusion models as general-purpose priors**: Beyond generation, diffusion models are increasingly applied to segmentation, depth estimation, inpainting, change detection, and anomaly detection — serving as learned priors for diverse visual tasks.
- **Autoregressive models challenge diffusion for interactive generation**: MineWorld (Guo et al., 2025) demonstrates that autoregressive Transformers with joint visual-action tokenization outperform diffusion-based approaches (Oasis) on both visual quality and controllability for real-time interactive world modeling. Combined with parallel decoding for >3× speedup, this suggests autoregressive models may be better suited than diffusion for interactive applications requiring fast, controllable frame generation. However, Yume1.5 (Mao et al., CVPR 2026) challenges this by achieving 12 FPS at 540p with a diffusion-based DiT through context compression (TSCM) and 4-step distillation — suggesting that the autoregressive vs. diffusion debate for interactive generation remains open.
- **Text-controlled event generation in video worlds**: Yume1.5 adds natural language event control to interactive world generation — enabling dynamic scene changes (e.g., "a ghost appeared") alongside keyboard-based camera/character movement. This extends video generation from purely visual control to semantic + spatial control, achieved with minimal event-specific data (4K curated videos) through decomposed text embedding.
- **Decoupled motion-appearance pipelines for efficient domain adaptation**: MAD (Rahimi et al., CVPR 2026) demonstrates that generalist video diffusion models can be efficiently adapted to domain-specific world models by decoupling motion forecasting from appearance synthesis. Rather than massive end-to-end fine-tuning (25K–50K GPU-hours), MAD uses two lightweight LoRA adapters on a single backbone — one to generate abstract pose videos, one to render photorealistic output conditioned on poses. All conditioning signals are projected through the base model's native VAE, eliminating new adapter architectures. This achieves competitive or superior quality at <6% compute cost, establishing a general methodology for adapting future VGMs to specialized domains. The targeted noise injection during Appearance Synthesizer training (adding latent noise only to skeleton regions) addresses the train-inference domain gap in cascaded generation pipelines.
- **Geometry-grounded memory for long-term consistent video generation**: Video World Models with Long-term Spatial Memory (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2025) augments video diffusion models with a three-part memory system — working memory (recent context frames), spatial memory (persistent static 3D point cloud via TSDF fusion), and episodic memory (sparse historical keyframes via cross-attention). The spatial memory is incrementally built using CUT3R for online 4D reconstruction, with TSDF fusion filtering out dynamic elements. On reversed trajectory revisits, this achieves PSNR 19.10 vs. 12.16 for the best baseline, demonstrating that explicit 3D memory enables far better scene consistency than 2D context windows alone. Built on CogVideoX-5B with ControlNet-style point cloud conditioning and historical cross-attention for keyframe retrieval.
- **Autoregressive temporal imputation for long-range generation**: Streetscapes (Deng et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) introduces temporal imputation — replacing random noise inputs with noised copies of previously generated frames — to prevent quality drift in autoregressive video diffusion. This enables generation of consistent street view sequences spanning multiple city blocks, far beyond what standard video diffusion models can produce. Combined with G-buffer conditioning from overhead maps (semantics, disparity, height), the system provides layout and camera control at city scale. The key insight that noised condition copies are more robust than clean conditioning is general and applicable beyond street view to any autoregressive diffusion video setting.
- **External memory enables editable and multiplayer diffusion game engines**: MultiGen (Po et al., 2026) decomposes diffusion game engines into Memory (2D map + poses), Observation (diffusion UNet conditioned on ray-traced disparity), and Dynamics (transformer pose predictor) modules. This modular design with explicit external memory enables two capabilities absent from monolithic next-frame predictors: user-authored level design from coarse 2D layouts, and real-time multiplayer via shared memory — each player runs an independent model instance reading/writing the same state. Trained entirely on single-player VizDoom data, the system generates consistent cross-viewpoint multiplayer interactions at ~20 FPS per player, with 75.4% opponent-presence accuracy vs. 65.3% for a split-screen baseline.
- **Video generation models as robot data engines**: DreamGen (Jang et al., NVIDIA, 2025) repurposes state-of-the-art video generation models (WAN2.1) as synthetic data generators for robot learning. LoRA fine-tuning on teleoperation data adapts the model to specific robot embodiments while preserving internet video priors, and the generated videos are paired with pseudo-actions via inverse dynamics models to form "neural trajectories." Log-linear scaling with synthetic data volume (up to 333× augmentation) and zero-to-one behavior/environment generalization demonstrate that video generation quality directly translates to downstream policy performance. DreamGen Bench provides a video-generation-specific evaluation that correlates with robotics outcomes, bridging the gap between video generation research and embodied AI applications.
- **Omnimodal unification of understanding and generation**: Cosmos 3 (NVIDIA, 2026) demonstrates that a single Mixture-of-Transformers architecture can achieve SOTA on both image generation (#1 open-source on Artificial Analysis) and video generation (best open-source on PAIBench-G) while simultaneously supporting multimodal understanding, audio generation, and action prediction. The dual-tower MoT design — autoregressive tokens for reasoning and diffusion tokens for generation sharing attention but using independent parameters, both initialized from pretrained VLM weights — enables joint training without capability trade-offs. Post-training specialization from the shared checkpoint (Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image, Cosmos3-Super-Image2Video) matches or exceeds dedicated models, suggesting that omnimodal pretraining provides a strong foundation for generation tasks. This challenges the assumption that peak generation quality requires specialized, single-purpose architectures.
- **From single-agent to multi-agent collaborative generation**: ShareVerse (Zhu et al., 2026) extends video generation from isolated single-perspective synthesis to collaborative multi-agent rendering of shared environments. Cross-agent collaborative attention and spatiotemporal memory retrieval maintain global consistency without explicit 3D representations, opening video generation to multi-agent simulation applications.
- **Unified multi-agent multi-view video generation**: MultiWorld (Wu et al., 2026) demonstrates that a single diffusion model (Wan2.2-5B backbone) can generate action-conditioned multi-view videos for multiple agents simultaneously. By decomposing multi-view generation into parallel single-view tasks conditioned on a shared 3D-aware global state (from frozen VGGT), inference latency remains nearly constant regardless of view count — achieving ~1.5× speedup over sequential generation. RoPE-based agent identity embedding and adaptive action weighting enable precise multi-agent controllability within the generation process.

## Open questions

- How to reduce sampling steps (T=1000 in DDPM) without sacrificing sample quality — addressed by subsequent work (DDIM, DPM-Solver) but the speed-quality trade-off remains active.
- The fundamental tension between sample quality (FID) and log-likelihood: whether architectures or objectives exist that can optimize both simultaneously.
- How to scale video diffusion models to high resolution and long duration without cascaded pipelines. Streetscapes demonstrates that temporal imputation enables long-range autoregressive generation without drift, and SPMem shows that explicit 3D spatial memory enables scene recall during revisits — but both operate at limited resolution and neither achieves perfect consistency.
- Whether interactive frame-by-frame generation can scale to high resolutions and real-time inference while maintaining visual quality and controllability.
- Whether principled methods for video data curation can replace heuristic thresholds (optical flow, aesthetic scores, text density), and how curation strategies transfer across domains.
- Whether MAD's decoupled motion-appearance paradigm generalizes beyond driving to other domains requiring structured dynamics (robotics, sports, indoor environments), and whether the intermediate representation (skeleton poses) transfers or needs domain-specific redesign.
- How to maintain spatiotemporal consistency across multiple distributed generation agents — three paradigms emerge: ShareVerse uses cross-agent attention without explicit 3D, MultiWorld uses a frozen 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) for shared global state, and MultiGen uses explicit external memory (2D map + poses) with independent per-player model instances. Whether any approach scales to many agents (10+) and diverse open-world domains remains open.
