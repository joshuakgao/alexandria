## Overview

Multi-agent systems research studies how multiple agents — whether AI-AI or human-AI — interact, coordinate, and collaborate in shared environments. Two foundational challenges define the field: partner modeling and communication. Carroll et al. (NeurIPS 2019) demonstrated the partner modeling problem concretely in cooperative Overcooked: self-play agents that achieved high rewards with copies of themselves performed dramatically worse when paired with human partners, while agents trained with even crude human models coordinated significantly better. Yu et al. (NeurIPS 2022) established that MAPPO — PPO with a centralized value function — achieves surprisingly competitive or superior performance to off-policy methods (QMix, MADDPG, value decomposition) across four cooperative benchmarks (MPE, SMAC, GRF, Hanabi) with minimal tuning, overturning the belief that on-policy methods are too sample-inefficient for multi-agent settings. CoDreamer (Toledo & Prorok, RLC 2024) addresses the communication problem by extending DreamerV3 to multi-agent settings with GNN-based communication at two levels — within world models (for environment modeling) and within actor-critic networks (for cooperation) — showing that independent world models fundamentally cannot capture inter-agent dependent dynamics, and that learned communication significantly improves sample efficiency and performance in cooperative tasks.

## Trends

- **Human-aware training for cooperation**: Self-play produces agents that coordinate only with copies of themselves. Incorporating learned human models (even simple behavior-cloned ones) into training dramatically improves human-AI coordination, establishing partner modeling as a requirement for cooperative settings. FCP (Strouse et al., NeurIPS 2021) goes further, showing that a diverse pool of self-play agents and their checkpoints — without any human data — produces even better human collaborators than behavioral cloning play.
- **Asymmetry between competition and cooperation**: In competitive games, partner suboptimality is advantageous; in cooperative games, it causes catastrophic coordination failure. This fundamental asymmetry means methods proven in competitive settings (AlphaGo, OpenAI Five) do not transfer directly to cooperative tasks.
- **Partner diversity over partner fidelity**: FCP demonstrates that the key ingredient for zero-shot human coordination is not accurate human models but diverse training partners. Random seed variation provides convention diversity (symmetry breaking), while training checkpoints provide skill-level diversity — and the latter comes at zero extra training cost.

- **PPO as a strong cooperative MARL baseline**: Yu et al. (NeurIPS 2022) demonstrate that MAPPO matches or exceeds off-policy SOTA (QMix, RODE, MADDPG) across MPE, SMAC (23 maps), Google Research Football (6 scenarios), and Hanabi (2–5 players) — all with comparable sample efficiency. The key finding is that multi-agent non-stationarity requires conservative PPO configurations: fewer training epochs (5–10 vs. single-agent's 30+), no mini-batch splitting, and small clipping ε (<0.2). Centralized value functions become critical as agent count grows (MAPPO vs. IPPO gap widens in 5-player Hanabi). This paper established MAPPO as the community-standard cooperative MARL baseline, directly adopted by CoDreamer and referenced across subsequent MARL work.
- **GNN-based communication for decentralized world models**: CoDreamer (Toledo & Prorok, 2024) introduces two-level communication within DreamerV3: world model communication improves environment modeling (capturing inter-agent dynamics that independent models miss), while actor-critic communication improves cooperative policy learning. The approach is strictly more expressive than independent learning — it can model true Dec-MDP dynamics. Benefits are most pronounced in pixel-based, partially observable settings.
- **Model-based RL for multi-agent sample efficiency**: Extending single-agent model-based methods (Dreamer) to multi-agent settings addresses the amplified sample efficiency problem when multiple agents must learn shared policies. Both IDreamer and CoDreamer significantly outperform model-free IPPO, especially with high-dimensional observations.
- **Collaborative video generation for shared worlds**: ShareVerse (Zhu et al., 2026) demonstrates that distributed video generation agents can collaboratively synthesize a globally consistent virtual environment. Cross-agent collaborative attention resolves visual conflicts in overlapping regions, while a spatiotemporal memory cache ensures environmental permanence across asynchronous agent trajectories — all without explicit 3D representations. This extends the multi-agent coordination problem from decision-making to perception and world synthesis.
- **Implicit communication through shared latent space**: Unlike CoDreamer's explicit GNN-based message passing, ShareVerse's cross-agent attention operates on geometrically-anchored latent features, enabling implicit spatial coordination. This suggests an alternative to explicit communication protocols for multi-agent consistency.
- **Scalable multi-agent world modeling via identity embedding and adaptive weighting**: MultiWorld (Wu et al., 2026) demonstrates that RoPE-based Agent Identity Embedding resolves the symmetry problem in multi-agent action conditioning (mirror actions become distinguishable), while Adaptive Action Weighting dynamically prioritizes active agents over stationary ones. Self-attention over agent action tokens explicitly models inter-agent interactions. This approach scales to variable agent counts without architectural changes — unlike CoDreamer's fixed GNN topology or ShareVerse's concatenated agent features.
- **CTDE dominates across game genres**: Li et al.'s comprehensive MARL survey (IEEE ToG, 2025) confirms that Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution is the prevailing paradigm across competitive games (StarCraft II, Dota 2), sports games (Rocket League, Google Research Football), and FPS games (Quake III). Self-play remains the most successful competitive training technique, while reward shaping and credit assignment remain the key unsolved challenges for cooperative settings. The survey proposes a five-dimensional game complexity framework for systematic cross-game comparison of MARL difficulty.
- **External memory for multiplayer diffusion game engines**: MultiGen (Po et al., 2026) takes a fundamentally different approach to multi-agent world generation than ShareVerse's cross-agent attention or MultiWorld's shared global state encoder. Instead of learning multi-agent consistency, MultiGen externalizes shared state: a persistent 2D map and player pose set serves as memory that all players read from and write to. Each player runs an independent Observation/Dynamics model instance — trained entirely on single-player data — with multiplayer consistency emerging from the shared external state rather than architectural coupling. This achieves 75.4% opponent-presence accuracy (vs. 65.3% for a split-screen baseline) and scales to arbitrary player counts without model changes, at ~20 FPS per player. The key insight: explicit structured state is sufficient for cross-player consistency in environments with well-defined geometry — no learned cross-agent attention required.
- **Theory-of-Mind via factorized world models**: Dreaming of Others (Leroy-Stone, WMW 2026) proposes factorizing the RSSM latent state into environment and teammate components, with an auxiliary ToM head that infers partner behavior embeddings from partial trajectories. This reframes MARL non-stationarity as a world modeling problem: rather than treating teammates as noise, the world model explicitly simulates their behavior, enabling zero-shot coordination through imagined rollouts with sampled teammate latents. While currently a proposal without empirical results, it bridges the Dreamer world model lineage with Theory-of-Mind agent modeling — addressing the partner modeling gap identified by Carroll et al. without requiring centralized training, shared imagination (MA-Dreamer), or explicit communication (CoDreamer).
- **Meta-learned Theory of Mind for agent modeling**: Machine Theory of Mind (Rabinowitz et al., ICML 2018) introduced the ToMnet — a neural network observer that uses meta-learning to build models of other agents from behavior alone. By separating character (stable traits) from mental state (episode-specific beliefs), the ToMnet performs few-shot inverse RL, distinguishes agent species, and passes false-belief tests (Sally-Anne). This foundational work framed agent modeling as meta-learning rather than hand-crafted Bayesian inference, directly influencing subsequent work on opponent modeling and cooperative coordination.
- **Situated ToM with asymmetric knowledge for collaborative tasks**: MindCraft (Bara et al., EMNLP 2021) extends multi-agent collaboration to settings where partners have asymmetric knowledge and skills, requiring explicit theory-of-mind reasoning and dialogue to establish common ground. Unlike ToMnet's passive behavioral observation, MindCraft shows that active communication is essential for inferring partner goals — partners who communicate less disagree more on mental states.
- **LLM agents as zero-shot multi-agent collaborators with emergent ToM**: Li et al. (EMNLP 2023) show that GPT-4 agents achieve MAPPO-competitive performance in a cooperative bomb-defusal task without any training, exhibiting emergent leadership, task allocation, and high-order ToM reasoning. Explicit belief state prompting halves the rounds to completion (12.3 vs. 28.3) — suggesting that LLMs' multi-agent coordination failures stem from context management rather than reasoning deficits. This contrasts with the MARL paradigm (MAPPO, CoDreamer) where coordination must be learned through millions of training steps.
- **Organizational structure for scalable LLM-agent teams**: Guo et al. (2024) show that unstructured communication among 3+ LLM agents leads to chaos (redundancy, interruption, conflicting messages), and that prompt-based hierarchical organization with designated leadership improves efficiency by up to 30%. Their Criticize-Reflect framework uses LLMs to meta-optimize organizational prompts, discovering novel team structures automatically. Human leaders still significantly outperform AI leaders, highlighting a gap in strategic coordination. This extends the LLM multi-agent paradigm from Li et al.'s broadcast-only 3-agent setting to flexible, optimizable organizational structures.
- **Proactive cooperation via LLM intention inference**: ProAgent (Zhang et al., AAAI 2024) outperforms five MARL baselines (SP, PBT, FCP, MEP, COLE) at zero-shot coordination in Overcooked-AI by using LLM reasoning to predict teammate intentions and iteratively correcting beliefs against observed behavior. Achieves >10% improvement over SOTA with human proxy models. Unlike FCP's diversity-based approach (no partner modeling) or CoDreamer's learned communication, ProAgent coordinates through explicit natural-language intention inference — fully interpretable and requiring no training. Validates that proactive intention modeling is more effective than reactive coordination for human-AI teaming.

## Open questions

- How to build human models that generalize across individuals, tasks, and interaction histories — FCP sidesteps this by avoiding human models entirely, but whether diversity-based approaches scale beyond gridworld is unknown
- Whether combining FCP-style diversity training with online adaptation during interaction can further improve coordination
- Scaling human-aware coordination to complex, continuous, real-world tasks beyond gridworld environments
- How to handle the diversity of human strategies without requiring per-person training data
- Whether theory-of-mind architectures (ToMnet's meta-learned agent models, Dreaming of Others' factorized latents) can replace behavior cloning for more robust partner modeling — and whether they scale beyond gridworlds to complex, high-dimensional domains
- Scaling communicative world models to heterogeneous agents and larger agent populations
- How to update communication topology during imagination rollouts — CoDreamer's fixed adjacency matrix may degrade over longer horizons
- Whether learned communication protocols transfer across environments or must be retrained per-task
- How to scale collaborative world synthesis beyond two agents — ShareVerse's cross-agent attention concatenates agent features (quadratic), MultiWorld's RoPE-based identity embedding and parallel view generation offers a more scalable path but is only tested up to 4 agents, and MultiGen's independent instances with shared external memory scales linearly but requires well-structured state
- Whether implicit coordination (ShareVerse's shared latent attention), explicit messaging (CoDreamer's GNN), shared global state conditioning (MultiWorld's VGGT-based GSE), or explicit external memory (MultiGen's 2D map + poses) is most effective for different multi-agent tasks — the right approach likely depends on whether the environment has well-defined structure (MultiGen) vs. open-ended visual complexity (ShareVerse/MultiWorld)
- How to transfer MARL coordination strategies learned in game environments to open-ended real-world multi-agent tasks — the gap between controlled game settings and unstructured real-world interaction remains significant
