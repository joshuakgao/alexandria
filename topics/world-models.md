## Overview

# World Models — Overview

*Last updated: 2026-07-04 | Sources: 10 papers*

## Current thesis

World models are learnable and measurable at scale through self-supervised prediction, and moreover, can be acquired from minimal naturalistic data with developmental plausibility — and even from unlabelled internet video without any action annotations. The foundational idea — Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) — established that agents can learn compressed spatial-temporal representations of environments (VAE + MDN-RNN), train compact policies using these representations, and even learn entirely inside hallucinated "dream" environments generated by the world model. Dreamer (Hafner et al., ICLR 2020) operationalized dream training at scale by introducing differentiable actor-critic learning in RSSM latent space, DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., ICLR 2021) closed the model-based vs. model-free gap on Atari via categorical latents and KL balancing, and DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., Nature 2025) achieved domain-general RL — outperforming specialized experts across 150+ tasks with fixed hyperparameters and collecting diamonds in Minecraft from scratch via scale-invariant robustness techniques. VPT (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022) demonstrated a complementary path: rather than learning an explicit dynamics model, it uses an inverse dynamics model to pseudo-label ~70K hours of unlabeled internet Minecraft video, producing a 0.5B-parameter behavioral prior that encodes implicit world knowledge — enabling diamond pickaxe crafting when fine-tuned with RL. Genie (Bruce et al., ICML 2024) removes the action-label requirement entirely, discovering latent actions from 30K hours of internet gameplay video via unsupervised VQ-VAE and scaling to 11B parameters — creating the first foundation world model that bridges passive video generation and interactive control. V-JEPA (2025) shows that intuitive physics emerges from representation-space prediction without explicit supervision. VBVR (2026) benchmarks world reasoning at scale across five cognitive capabilities. BabyZWM (2026) demonstrates that data-efficient zero-shot visual-cognitive abilities emerge from just 868 hours of egocentric child video (3 months of waking experience) using sparse temporally-factored prediction and approximate causal inference. Together, these papers establish: (1) representation-space prediction is a sufficient inductive bias for physics understanding, (2) diverse reasoning capabilities generalize systematically when benchmarked at scale, (3) architectural choices (factorization, causal inference) enable learning from human-scale data while matching developmental trajectories and neural alignment with infant brains, (4) action supervision is not necessary — interactive world models can emerge from passive video observation alone, and (5) implicit world knowledge encoded in behavioral priors can be as effective as explicit dynamics models for enabling complex sequential behavior.

## Key trends

**2018 — Foundation:**
- **Modular V-M-C architecture**: Ha & Schmidhuber introduce the separation of Vision (VAE), Memory (MDN-RNN), and Controller (linear policy) — allowing the world model to absorb representational complexity while keeping the policy minimal (867 parameters). This modularity becomes a recurring design pattern in subsequent work.
- **Dream training**: First demonstration that an agent can be trained entirely inside a hallucinated environment generated by its own learned world model, and transfer the policy to reality. Establishes the paradigm of using world models as simulators for policy learning.
- **Stochastic world models for robustness**: The use of mixture density networks with adjustable temperature to model environment stochasticity prevents adversarial exploitation of deterministic models — a key insight that prefigures later work on model uncertainty in planning.
- **Compressed latent dynamics**: Learning to predict in latent space (z vectors) rather than pixel space enables efficient temporal modeling. This anticipates V-JEPA's representation-space prediction and DeltaTok's temporal compression.

**2020:**
- **Differentiable actor-critic in learned latent dynamics**: Dreamer (Hafner et al., ICLR 2020) operationalizes dream training at scale by replacing derivative-free optimization (CEM/CMA-ES) with analytic gradient propagation through learned RSSM dynamics. The actor-critic approach — value model predicts λ-returns, action model maximizes values via backpropagation through the dynamics — solves long-horizon tasks that pure reward-horizon methods cannot. This is the first demonstration that neural network world models are accurate enough for gradient-based policy optimization from pixels at scale (20 DM Control tasks).
- **RSSM as the standard world model architecture**: The Recurrent State Space Model (deterministic recurrent + stochastic latent states) from PlaNet/Dreamer becomes the dominant architecture for learned dynamics, used in subsequent work (DreamerV2, DreamerV3, IRIS). The combination of deterministic memory (for long-term dependencies) and stochastic components (for multimodal futures) provides accurate multi-step predictions in compact latent space.
- **Value estimation beyond imagination horizon**: Dreamer's λ-returns (Vλ) — exponentially-weighted averages of k-step returns with value bootstrapping — make the agent robust to imagination horizon length. This resolves the fundamental shortsightedness of prior model-based methods that could only optimize rewards within a fixed planning horizon.

**2021:**
- **Categorical latents for accurate world models**: DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., ICLR 2021) replaces Gaussian latent variables with 32 categorical variables × 32 classes, producing sparse binary state vectors that better capture non-smooth environment dynamics (room transitions, object appearance/disappearance). This is the key architectural change that enables the first world model agent to achieve human-level Atari performance.
- **KL balancing for stable latent dynamics learning**: Asymmetric learning rates (80% prior, 20% posterior) in the KL loss prevent the common failure mode where a poorly trained prior degrades representation quality. By prioritizing prior accuracy, the transition model becomes reliable enough for long-horizon imagination — a general technique applicable to any sequential VAE.
- **General representations outperform reward-specific ones**: Ablations show that stopping reward gradients is neutral or beneficial — the world model learns better representations from image reconstruction alone. This connects to V-JEPA's later finding that self-supervised prediction without task-specific objectives produces generalizable representations.
- **10,000× imagination amplification**: DreamerV2 imagines 468B compact states from 50M real interactions at 200M Atari frames, demonstrating that the data efficiency of model-based RL comes from massive experience amplification in latent space, not just from better representations.

**2022:**
- **Inverse dynamics as implicit world knowledge extraction**: VPT (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022) demonstrates that a non-causal inverse dynamics model trained on just ~2K hours of labeled gameplay can pseudo-label ~70K hours of unlabeled internet Minecraft video, enabling a 0.5B-parameter behavioral cloning foundation model with nontrivial zero-shot capabilities. The IDM is two orders of magnitude more data-efficient than direct BC because inverting environment mechanics (a world modeling task) is far easier than modeling the full distribution of human intent. The resulting behavioral prior encodes implicit world knowledge — the model learns terrain navigation, inventory management, and multi-step crafting sequences (~970 consecutive actions for a crafting table) without any explicit dynamics model. This establishes a middle ground between Genie's fully unsupervised latent actions and NitroGen's automatically extracted action labels: minimal human labeling (~$2K USD) unlocks internet-scale video for action learning.
- **Behavioral priors as exploration priors for world model RL**: VPT's three-phase training (pretraining → BC fine-tuning → RL fine-tuning) achieves diamond pickaxe crafting in Minecraft — requiring ~24,000 consecutive actions with the native human interface — a task impossible for RL from scratch. The KL divergence loss to the frozen pretrained policy during RL fine-tuning prevents catastrophic forgetting of latent skills, enabling progressive discovery through the technology tree. This complements DreamerV3's explicit world model approach to Minecraft diamonds: DreamerV3 learns an explicit dynamics model and plans in latent space, while VPT learns an implicit behavioral prior from human video and fine-tunes with RL. Both achieve diamond-level results, suggesting that implicit and explicit world knowledge are complementary paths.

**2023:**
- **Scale-invariant world model learning for domain-general RL**: DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., Nature 2025) introduces robustness techniques — symlog predictions, symexp twohot distributions, percentile return normalization, and free bits — that decouple gradient magnitudes from target magnitudes across all model components. This eliminates per-domain hyperparameter tuning, enabling a single configuration to outperform specialized experts across 150+ tasks in 8 domains. The key architectural insight is that scale invariance (not domain-specific architecture) is the bottleneck for general world model learning.
- **Minecraft diamond from scratch**: DreamerV3 is the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft without human data or curricula — requiring ~20K-step sequential plans with sparse terminal rewards in a procedurally generated 3D open world. This demonstrates that RSSM-based world models can learn long-horizon strategies in environments far more complex than the control suites and Atari games of prior work.
- **Predictable scaling laws for world model RL**: Larger DreamerV3 models (12M→400M parameters) consistently achieve higher scores with less environment interaction, offering a clear compute-performance trade-off. This parallels scaling laws in language modeling and suggests world model RL can benefit systematically from increased compute.
- **Completion of the Dreamer arc**: V1 (differentiable imagination) → V2 (categorical latents, KL balancing) → V3 (scale-invariant robustness) represents a complete progression from domain-specific to domain-general world model RL, with each generation addressing the key failure mode of the previous one.

**2023 — Autoregressive video world models for autonomous driving:**
- **Next-token prediction as world modeling**: GAIA-1 (Hu et al., Wayve, 2023) recasts world modeling as autoregressive next-token prediction over discretized video frames, using a 6.5B parameter transformer. By factoring the system into a world model (token-space dynamics) and a video diffusion decoder (pixel rendering), each component scales independently. This bridges the gap between world models (which learn dynamics but produce low-fidelity outputs) and generative video models (which produce realistic video but lack structured future prediction).
- **Multimodal conditioning for controllable generation**: GAIA-1 accepts video, text (via T5), and action (speed + curvature) inputs through interleaved token sequences, enabling fine-grained control over both ego-vehicle behavior and scene features (weather, traffic lights, objects). Conditioning dropout during training (20% unconditioned / 40% action / 40% text) enables flexible generation modes at inference.
- **Semantic tokenization via DINO distillation**: The image tokenizer uses DINO feature regression to guide discrete tokens toward semantic rather than high-frequency representations. This makes the sequence prediction problem more tractable — the world model learns to predict semantically meaningful transitions rather than raw pixel patterns.
- **Scaling laws transfer from language to video world models**: Power-law fits from models 10,000× to 10× smaller accurately predict GAIA-1's final cross-entropy, demonstrating that LLM-like scaling laws hold for video world models. This establishes a clear compute-performance trade-off for world model development.
- **Emergent 3D understanding without explicit supervision**: Despite training only on monocular video with next-token prediction, GAIA-1 exhibits 3D geometry understanding (pitch/roll from speed bumps), reactive agent behaviors (other cars responding to ego actions), and out-of-distribution generalization (driving off-road) — suggesting that sufficiently large autoregressive models learn implicit world structure from video alone.

**2024 — City-scale generative world models:**
- **Autoregressive video diffusion for city-scale scene generation**: Streetscapes (Deng et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) generates long-range, 3D-consistent street view sequences spanning multiple city blocks by combining a layout-conditioned two-frame video diffusion model with a novel temporal imputation method for autoregressive generation. The key insight is that using noised copies of condition frames (temporal imputation) prevents quality drift far more effectively than conditioning on clean frames directly — a general principle for autoregressive diffusion generation. G-buffer conditioning (semantics, disparity, height maps rendered from overhead maps) provides coarse geometric control without requiring precise 3D models or per-object annotations. Trained on Google Street View data from 23 cities, the system supports text-conditioned style control (weather, time of day, city style) and controllable camera trajectories. This bridges world models and video generation at city scale — generating explorable environments from map data rather than requiring explicit 3D reconstruction.

**2024:**
- **Communicative world models for multi-agent RL**: CoDreamer (Toledo & Prorok, RLC 2024) extends DreamerV3 to multi-agent settings by adding GNN-based communication within the RSSM. Independent world models cannot capture inter-agent dependent dynamics (a formal expressiveness limitation), so CoDreamer introduces two-level communication: within world models for environment modeling and within actor-critic networks for cooperation. This is the first integration of learned communication into the Dreamer world model architecture and significantly outperforms independent multi-agent Dreamer (IDreamer) and model-free IPPO.
- **World models as social simulators**: Dreaming of Others (Leroy-Stone, WMW 2026) proposes factorizing the RSSM latent into environment and teammate components, with a Theory-of-Mind head that infers partner behavior from partial trajectories. Unlike CoDreamer's explicit communication, this approach models teammates as structured latent processes within a single agent's world model — requiring no shared imagination or centralized training. During imagination, sampled teammate latents condition the actor-critic, enabling zero-shot and few-shot coordination with unseen partners. This is a conceptual proposal (no empirical results) but represents a natural extension of world models from simulating physical dynamics to simulating social behavior.
- **Unsupervised latent action discovery from video**: Genie (Bruce et al., ICML 2024) demonstrates that frame-level interactive control can be learned from unlabelled internet video alone, without any action annotations. The latent action model (LAM) discovers a consistent set of 8 discrete actions via VQ-VAE that remain semantically stable across different inputs — effectively learning a "controller" from pure observation. This fills the gap between passive video models (text-conditioned, video-level control) and traditional world models (action-labelled, frame-level control), creating a new model class: generative interactive environments.
- **Foundation world model from internet video**: At 11B parameters trained on 30K hours of 2D platformer gameplay, Genie is the first foundation world model trained without action labels. Scaling analysis shows graceful improvement from 40M to 11B parameters, paralleling scaling laws observed in language models. Unlike Ha & Schmidhuber's World Models (which require environment interaction to collect training data), Genie leverages passive internet video — vastly expanding the data frontier.
- **Latent actions as a transfer interface**: Genie's latent action space transfers to unseen environments: a behavioral cloning policy using LAM-inferred actions matches oracle performance on CoinRun with only 200 expert action-mapping samples. This opens the path to training agents from unlabelled video demonstrations — the LAM provides the "action labels" that supervised approaches require.
- **Arbitrary image prompting for environment generation**: Genie can be prompted with text-to-image outputs, hand-drawn sketches, or real-world photographs to generate interactive environments, demonstrating out-of-distribution generalization characteristic of foundation models. This enables unlimited environment generation for agent training without requiring access to actual game engines or simulators.

**2024:**
- **Feature prediction as self-supervised world modeling objective**: V-JEPA (Bardes et al., ICLR 2025) establishes that predicting masked video representations in latent space — without pixel reconstruction, text, or pretrained encoders — produces versatile frozen encoders that outperform all prior video methods on both motion and appearance tasks. The JEPA framework (encoder + narrow predictor + EMA target) prevents collapse while forcing the encoder to capture maximal information about video dynamics. Multi-block masking (~90% ratio, spatially continuous blocks across full temporal extent) creates a hard prediction task that yields semantically rich representations. V-JEPA's success validates representation-space prediction as a principled middle ground between pixel-level reconstruction (which wastes capacity on low-level detail) and contrastive methods (which require hand-crafted augmentations). The intuitive physics paper (Ballas et al., 2025) later showed that these same V-JEPA representations exhibit emergent physics understanding without any explicit physics supervision.

**2025:**
- **Emergent physics from self-supervised learning**: No physics labels, no task-specific training—just masked video prediction. Physics understanding emerges as a byproduct of learning to model video. Challenges "core knowledge" hypothesis and supports learned emergence.
- **Violation-of-expectation as universal probe**: Adapting developmental psychology's surprise metric enables zero-shot physics probing without task-specific adaptation. "Surprise" (prediction error) directly measures whether a model's world model expects observed events.
- **Minimal data sufficiency**: Physics understanding achieves above-chance accuracy with as little as one week of video. Suggests physics reasoning is a learnable signal accessible at modest data scales, not requiring massive pretraining.
- **Alignment with predictive coding neuroscience**: Results align with neuroscience theories (Rao & Ballard, Hohwy) that brains learn by minimizing prediction error. V-JEPA provides a computational instantiation of predictive coding for vision.

**2025 — Video world models as synthetic data generators for robotics:**
- **Neural trajectories as a scalable data axis for robot learning**: DreamGen (Jang et al., NVIDIA, 2025) demonstrates that video world models are more valuable as offline synthetic data generators than as real-time planners. The 4-stage pipeline — LoRA-fine-tune a video model (WAN2.1) on teleoperation data, generate rollouts from new initial frames + language instructions, label pseudo-actions via IDM or latent action model, train visuomotor policies on the resulting "neural trajectories" — achieves log-linear policy improvement with synthetic data volume (up to 333× augmentation). This directly builds on VPT's inverse dynamics insight and Genie's latent action discovery, but shifts the use case from behavioral priors or interactive environments to scalable data augmentation.
- **Zero-to-one behavior and environment generalization**: From teleoperation data covering only a single pick-and-place task in one environment, DreamGen enables a GR1 humanoid to perform 22 novel behaviors (pouring, tool use, articulated objects) at 43.2% success in seen environments and 28.5% in unseen environments — tasks where the base policy achieves 0%. This demonstrates that video world model priors (physical reasoning, language grounding) transfer to robot behaviors never seen during fine-tuning, a qualitatively different capability from data augmentation of seen tasks.
- **DreamGen Bench bridges video generation and robotics evaluation**: A benchmark evaluating how well video models adapt to novel robot embodiments (instruction following, physics following, generalization) shows strong correlation with downstream policy success — providing a diagnostic proxy that doesn't require a physical robot. This complements VBVR's cognitive benchmarking with embodiment-specific evaluation.
- **Embodiment-agnostic pipeline**: Validated across three robot platforms (Fourier GR1 humanoid, Franka Emika, SO-100) with consistent gains, demonstrating that the pipeline generalizes across embodiments — unlike TraceGen's geometry-focused transfer, DreamGen operates entirely in pixel space.

**2025 — Geometry-grounded memory for video world models:**
- **Human-inspired memory taxonomy for long-term consistent world generation**: Video World Models with Long-term Spatial Memory (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2025) introduces a three-part memory framework inspired by human cognition: working memory (recent context frames for motion continuity), spatial memory (a persistent global static point cloud built via TSDF fusion of CUT3R-reconstructed frames, filtering out dynamic elements), and episodic memory (sparse historical keyframes stored when new regions are revealed). The spatial memory is rendered from target camera poses and injected via a ControlNet-style condition DiT, while episodic memory is accessed via historical cross-attention. This directly addresses the forgetting problem in autoregressive world models — on reversed trajectory revisits, the method achieves PSNR 19.10 vs. best baseline 12.16, demonstrating that explicit 3D spatial memory enables far better scene recall than sliding-window context alone. The approach complements Streetscapes' temporal imputation (which prevents quality drift but does not maintain persistent scene memory) and Yume1.5's context compression (which extends effective context but still operates in 2D).

**2025 — Real-time interactive world models:**
- **Autoregressive Transformers outperform diffusion for interactive world models**: MineWorld (Guo et al., 2025) demonstrates that a visual-action autoregressive Transformer trained with next-token prediction on interleaved state-action tokens significantly outperforms the diffusion-based Oasis on both visual quality (FVD 227 vs. 377) and action controllability (F1 0.73 vs. 0.41) at comparable model scales. This extends GAIA-1's autoregressive world model paradigm from driving to gaming, with the key difference being joint state-action tokenization rather than separate conditioning.
- **Parallel decoding for real-time interaction**: MineWorld's diagonal decoding algorithm exploits spatial redundancy in image tokens to achieve >3× speedup over sequential autoregressive decoding, enabling 4–7 FPS generation. This directly addresses Genie's resolution and real-time inference bottleneck — demonstrating that autoregressive world models can be fast enough for real-time human interaction.
- **Controllability as a first-class evaluation metric**: MineWorld introduces IDM-based controllability metrics (action classification F1, camera movement L1) for world model evaluation, moving beyond visual quality alone. This complements visual metrics (FVD, PSNR) and addresses a gap in standardized world model evaluation.
- **Diffusion-based world models achieve real-time interaction via context compression and distillation**: Yume1.5 (Mao et al., CVPR 2026) demonstrates that diffusion models — not just autoregressive Transformers — can achieve real-time interactive world generation (12 FPS at 540p on a single A100). The key is Joint Temporal-Spatial-Channel Modeling (TSCM), which compresses historical context via dual-path processing (temporal-spatial compression through standard attention + channel compression through linear attention), achieving constant inference time regardless of video length. Combined with Self-Forcing distillation (4-step inference), Yume1.5 is 70× faster than prior interactive world models while achieving superior instruction following (0.836 vs. 0.657).
- **Text-controlled event generation in interactive worlds**: Yume1.5 introduces text-based event generation — dynamic scene changes triggered by natural language descriptions — as a new capability for interactive world models. By decomposing text prompts into event descriptions (processed once) and action descriptions (pre-cached), the model adds semantic control beyond keyboard/mouse inputs. This extends the expressiveness of interactive world models beyond what MineWorld and Genie offer.

**2026:**
- **Large-scale benchmarks for world reasoning**: VBVR introduces 2.015M samples—1000× larger than prior benchmarks. Enables first reproducible scaling studies of world reasoning across five cognitive capabilities.
- **Cognitive science grounding**: VBVR anchors task taxonomy in foundational faculties (Spatiality, Transformation, Knowledge, Abstraction, Perception) rather than ad-hoc task selection.
- **Verifiable evaluation paradigm**: VBVR-Bench uses rule-based, human-aligned scorers instead of VLM judges. Enables reproducible, interpretable diagnosis of specific reasoning bottlenecks.
- **Emergent generalization in world reasoning**: VBVR's scaling study reveals that models improve on unseen task types with more training data—suggesting world reasoning capabilities generalize systematically, not just memorize.
- **Integration of physics and task reasoning**: V-JEPA's emergent physics and VBVR's task-based benchmarks represent complementary approaches to understanding world models—mechanism vs. measurement.
- **Delta tokenization for efficient generative forecasting**: DeltaTok (Kerssies et al.) compresses inter-frame VFM feature differences into single continuous tokens — a 1,024× reduction at 512×512 — collapsing video from 3D spatiotemporal to 1D temporal sequences. Combined with Best-of-Many training (generating K hypotheses in parallel, supervising only the best), DeltaWorld achieves better dense forecasting accuracy than Cosmos with 35× fewer parameters and 2,000× fewer FLOPs. The key insight is that consecutive frames differ in low-dimensional ways, so predicting change is far cheaper than predicting full state. This complements SceneTok's spatial compression with temporal compression — together suggesting that the future of world modeling lies in extreme tokenization that discards redundant spatiotemporal structure.
- **Scene tokenization for generative world models**: SceneTok (Asim et al.) introduces the first 3D scene tokenizer — compressing multi-view observations into 1024 unstructured tokens (32K floats), 1–3 orders of magnitude smaller than Gaussian splatting or LVSM representations. The two-stage design (scene autoencoder + latent diffusion) decouples scene generation from view rendering, enabling 5-second generation and 32 views/second rendering. Critically, the generative rectified flow decoder handles uncertainty gracefully, adapting its sampling distribution based on how much information the tokens encode about a given view. This represents a shift from explicit 3D representations toward compressed latent world models that can be directly processed by generative transformers.
- **Data-efficient learning from naturalistic child video**: BabyZWM learns diverse visual-cognitive abilities (depth, motion, segmentation, intuitive physics) from just 868 hours of egocentric video. Challenges assumption that world models require massive datasets; opens path toward human-scale data efficiency.
- **Zero-shot extraction via approximate causal inference**: BabyZWM introduces a general interface for extracting task-specific predictions from a single trained predictor by comparing counterfactual inputs. Shifts from task-specific readout training to universal zero-shot prompting.
- **Developmental alignment and neural plausibility**: BabyZWM exhibits learning trajectories paralleling human infant development and internal representations correlating with fMRI responses. Provides evidence that architectural choices (sparse temporal factorization) capture cognitively and biologically plausible learning principles.
- **Internet-scale behavior cloning as implicit world modeling**: NitroGen (Magne et al., CVPR 2026) trains a vision-action foundation model on 40,000 hours of gameplay across 1,000+ games via behavior cloning from internet videos with automatically extracted action labels. The flow-matching action head generates 16-action chunks conditioned on single frames, learning implicit dynamics models across diverse game environments. Unlike explicit world models (V-JEPA, DeltaWorld), the world knowledge is entirely implicit in the policy — the model cannot predict future states, only generate appropriate actions. Yet transferable motor primitives emerge (combat, navigation skills transfer to unseen games with up to 52% improvement), suggesting the model captures reusable dynamics priors.
- **View graphs as empirical world models of view transitions**: ViewSuite (Wang et al., 2026) introduces the view graph — a compact map of how viewpoints connect across a scene, assembled entirely from the agent's on-policy self-exploration. Unlike learned latent world models (V-JEPA, DeltaWorld), the view graph is an explicit, structured representation that grows incrementally as the policy improves. Through task reformulation, any path (including from failed episodes) becomes valid planning supervision. This bridges world models and planning: the graph holds exactly what the model has experienced, and distillation turns it into internalized spatial priors that transfer to other view-understanding tasks.
- **3D point flows as unified state-action world models for manipulation**: PointWorld (Huang et al., CVPR 2026) represents both scene state and robot actions as 3D point flows, enabling a single pre-trained model to predict full-scene dynamics across diverse embodiments, objects, and tasks. Trained on ~2M trajectories (~500 hours) from real (DROID) and simulated (BEHAVIOR-1K) data using a PTv3 backbone with frozen DINOv3 features, it achieves real-time inference (0.1s) and zero-shot MPC-based manipulation on a real robot. This is a concrete instantiation of V-JEPA's "integration with action" open problem — an action-conditioned world model operating in 3D physical space rather than learned latent space, with scaling laws (log-linear gains with data and model size) paralleling those observed in language and 2D vision.
- **Adversarial curriculum for robust world model training**: PROWL (Güzel et al., 2026) introduces a constrained adversarial framework that inverts the typical RL-world-model relationship — instead of training a policy inside a world model, a KL-anchored adversarial policy actively discovers failure-inducing trajectories in the real environment, and the world model is iteratively refined on these examples via a Prioritized Adversarial Trajectory (PAT) buffer. Implemented on a 1.3B Wan2.1-based diffusion forcing world model in MineRL, PROWL improves over both passive pretraining and a matched-compute baseline across held-out BASALT tasks (-12.6% AFS-EPE), cross-buffer adversarial transfer (-8.9% AFS-EPE), and 27 strictly novel composite action modes. Critically, behavioral anchoring is essential: weakly constrained adversaries degenerate into camera-thrashing reward hacking, while properly KL-anchored adversaries expose failures that are both hard and learnable. This establishes that world models benefit from selectively generating informative training data, not just larger datasets.
- **Motion-appearance decoupling for efficient driving world models**: MAD (Rahimi et al., CVPR 2026) decouples driving world modeling into two sequential stages — a Motion Forecaster generates abstract skeletonized pose videos capturing scene dynamics, then an Appearance Synthesizer renders photorealistic RGB conditioned on these poses. Both stages reuse a single pretrained video backbone (SVD or LTX) with lightweight LoRA adapters, projecting all control signals through the model's native VAE. This "reasoning-then-rendering" paradigm achieves VISTA-competitive quality with <6% of its compute and, when scaled to LTX-13B, matches proprietary Cosmos Predict 2. Crucially, the abstract intermediate representation prevents mode collapse: by removing visual textures, the Motion Forecaster learns underlying dynamics rather than spurious correlations, maintaining trajectory diversity that naive fine-tuning destroys. This validates the principle that separating "what moves" from "what it looks like" is a powerful inductive bias for world model efficiency.
- **Trace-space world models for cross-embodiment transfer**: TraceGen (Lee et al., CVPR 2026) introduces a world model that predicts future motion in compact 3D trace space — scene-level 3D keypoint trajectories — rather than pixels. By abstracting away appearance and camera variation while retaining geometric structure, TraceGen enables learning from heterogeneous cross-embodiment videos (humans + diverse robots). The TraceForge data pipeline converts 123K videos into 1.8M observation–trace–language triplets via camera-motion compensation, depth estimation, and speed retargeting. With just five demonstrations, TraceGen achieves 80% success on real-world manipulation tasks and 67.5% from uncalibrated human phone videos (human→robot transfer), running 50–600× faster than video-generation-based world models. This advances PointWorld's 3D motion approach by eliminating the need for action conditioning entirely — the model predicts scene-level motion traces that are embodiment-agnostic, with a lightweight warm-up translating traces to specific robot action spaces.
- **External memory as the missing primitive for diffusion game engines**: MultiGen (Po et al., 2026) demonstrates that introducing an explicit external memory (2D map geometry + player poses) into a diffusion game engine enables both user-authored level design and real-time multiplayer interaction from the same architectural choice. The system decomposes generation into Memory (persistent map + poses), Observation (next-frame diffusion conditioned on ray-traced disparity from memory), and Dynamics (lightweight transformer for pose updates). Crucially, multiplayer emerges from single-player training: each player runs independent Observation/Dynamics instances reading/writing a shared memory, with cross-player consistency arising from the shared state rather than multi-view supervision. Running at ~20 FPS per player on A100, this is the first diffusion game engine to demonstrate real-time multiplayer with consistent cross-viewpoint interactions (player visibility, combat, death/respawn). Unlike SPMem's 3D point cloud memory (focused on view recall of static scenes), MultiGen's 2D map memory provides persistent structural reference for interactive gameplay.
- **Collaborative shared world modeling via distributed video generation**: ShareVerse (Zhu et al., 2026) demonstrates that multiple distributed agents can collaboratively synthesize a globally consistent virtual environment without explicit 3D representations. By combining geometrically-anchored latent features (via raymap injection), cross-agent collaborative attention, and a spatiotemporal memory cache, agents resolve visual conflicts in overlapping regions and maintain environmental permanence across asynchronous revisitations. This extends world models from single-observer to multi-agent shared reality — a prerequisite for applications like multiplayer simulation and multi-robot coordination.
- **Unified multi-agent multi-view world modeling**: MultiWorld (Wu et al., 2026) introduces a scalable framework that generates action-conditioned multi-view videos for multiple agents simultaneously. The Multi-Agent Condition Module uses RoPE-based Agent Identity Embedding to resolve agent identity ambiguity and Adaptive Action Weighting to prioritize active agents, while the Global State Encoder uses frozen VGGT to extract 3D-aware global state from partial observations for multi-view consistency. Unlike ShareVerse's distributed generation with cross-agent attention, MultiWorld decomposes multi-view simulation into parallel single-view tasks sharing a compact global state — enabling constant inference latency regardless of view count. This advances the ShareVerse paradigm by addressing both agent controllability and view scalability in a single unified framework.
- **Test-time adaptation for latent world models**: AdaJEPA (Wang et al., 2026) demonstrates that JEPA world models should not remain frozen at deployment. By performing self-supervised adaptation within the MPC loop — using the observed next-state transition as a prediction target after each executed action — a single gradient step per replan substantially improves planning success under visual, dynamics, shape, and layout distribution shifts. The approach is implementation-agnostic (consistent gains across global-feature, spatial-feature, and DINO-WM variants) and introduces negligible latency. This directly addresses the compounding model error problem: rather than building more accurate frozen models, AdaJEPA continuously recalibrates predictions during deployment, drawing on biological sensorimotor adaptation as inspiration.
- **Agents that generalize across world models**: SIMA 2 (Google DeepMind, 2025) demonstrates that a Gemini-based embodied agent trained across diverse 3D game environments can generalize to photorealistic worlds generated by Genie 3 — a generative world model. This validates the virtuous cycle between increasingly capable world models and increasingly capable agents: world models provide unlimited training environments, while generalist agents demonstrate that skills transfer across both hand-crafted and generated worlds. SIMA 2's open-ended self-improvement — using foundation models for task generation and reward scoring in novel Genie 3 environments — further demonstrates that agents can autonomously acquire new skills in generated worlds.

## Open problems

**Latent imagination for control (Dreamer):**
- **Compounding model errors**: Gradient-based imagination amplifies model inaccuracies over long horizons — while λ-returns mitigate this, scaling to complex real-world environments with rich visual detail remains challenging
- **Discrete action spaces**: Dreamer uses continuous actions with reparameterized gradients; extending differentiable imagination to discrete action domains required architectural changes (DreamerV2's straight-through gradients)
- **Multi-task and transfer**: Dreamer learns single-task world models; whether a single RSSM can support multi-task imagination across diverse environments is open
- **Representation learning objectives**: Reconstruction works well but allocates capacity to task-irrelevant visual details; contrastive and reward-only objectives are competitive but not consistently better — the optimal representation learning objective for world models remains debated

**Generative interactive environments (Genie):**
- **Resolution and real-time inference**: Genie operates at 160×90 resolution and ~1 FPS; MineWorld achieves 224×384 at 4–7 FPS via parallel decoding, and Yume1.5 reaches 540p at 12 FPS via TSCM + distillation — but scaling to full HD with high visual quality remains an open challenge
- **Beyond 2D platformers**: Genie is trained on 2D side-scrollers; whether unsupervised latent action discovery generalizes to 3D environments, continuous action spaces, or complex real-world scenes remains undemonstrated
- **Long-horizon coherence**: Autoregressive frame generation degrades over time; Streetscapes' temporal imputation prevents quality drift and SPMem's spatial memory enables scene recall during revisits, but even with explicit 3D memory, perfect view recall remains very challenging (PSNR 19.10 on revisits). Maintaining fully consistent environments over minutes rather than seconds is unsolved
- **Richer action spaces**: The 8-action discrete codebook limits expressiveness; discovering continuous or hierarchical latent action spaces from video is open
- **Text-conditioned generation**: Genie relies on image prompts; integrating text conditioning for interactive environments would dramatically expand usability

**Physics learning (V-JEPA):**
- **Real-world physics complexity**: IntPhys tests synthetic object trajectories; whether models capture complex real-world physics (soft bodies, friction, fluids, deformables) remains unexplored
- **Long-horizon prediction**: Current work focuses on short-term prediction (next few frames); whether models acquire stable physics models over extended horizons (>1 minute) is unclear
- **Counterfactual reasoning**: Current framework is passive (detecting violations); active reasoning—generating "what if?" scenarios—is not addressed
- **Semantic grounding**: Models learn visual physics but do not integrate semantic understanding (categories, materials, agents); fusion of physics and semantics is open
- **Scaling laws**: Study uses modest models; whether scaling to larger architectures reveals new physics behaviors is unknown
- **Integration with action**: PointWorld provides action-conditioned 3D point flow prediction for zero-shot manipulation; TraceGen removes the action-conditioning requirement entirely by predicting scene-level motion traces that are embodiment-agnostic. However, both operate on short horizons and require calibrated RGB-D; whether trace-space or point-flow approaches extend to long-horizon planning and RGB-only settings remains open
- **Implicit vs. explicit physics**: Do learned representations capture explicit constraints (e.g., Lagrangian mechanics) or only implicit patterns? PointWorld shows that 3D point flow prediction implicitly captures material properties, articulation, and gravity without explicit supervision, but interpretability of what is learned remains open
- **Cross-embodiment transfer via trace space**: TraceGen demonstrates that 3D trace representations enable human→robot transfer from uncalibrated phone videos (67.5% success), but with a gap vs. same-embodiment warm-up (80%). Whether trace-space abstraction scales to dexterous manipulation, deformable objects, and bimanual tasks — where fine-grained embodiment differences matter more — is untested
- **Active data generation vs. passive data scaling**: PROWL demonstrates that adversarial curriculum training improves world models beyond what matched-compute passive fine-tuning achieves, suggesting dataset composition matters more than size. However, PROWL is evaluated only in MineRL (discrete actions, first-person view); whether adversarial discovery scales to richer environments (driving, robotics) with continuous action spaces and higher-dimensional observations is open

**Multi-agent shared world modeling (ShareVerse, MultiWorld, MultiGen):**
- **Scaling beyond two agents**: ShareVerse demonstrates two-agent scenarios; MultiWorld scales to 2–4 agents via RoPE-based identity embeddings and adaptive action weighting; MultiGen supports arbitrary player counts via independent per-player model instances sharing external memory — but whether any approach extends efficiently to 10+ agents with complex interactions is untested
- **Domain generalization**: ShareVerse is demonstrated only in autonomous driving (CARLA); MultiWorld shows generalization across video games (ItTakesTwo) and robotics (RoboFactory), but open-world environments remain untested
- **Real-world deployment**: Both require known camera setups; integrating with real-time localization and handling pose uncertainty is unaddressed
- **Memory management at scale**: The spatiotemporal memory cache grows unboundedly; efficient forgetting or compression strategies for long-horizon, large-scale worlds are needed
- **Multi-view consistency at scale**: MultiWorld's Global State Encoder (frozen VGGT) compresses multi-view observations into 3D-aware global state for cross-view consistency; whether this approach maintains quality with many views (10+) or diverse camera configurations is open
- **Resolution scaling**: MultiWorld operates at 320×320 per view; scaling multi-agent multi-view generation to higher resolutions while maintaining consistency and controllability is unsolved

**Test-time adaptation (AdaJEPA):**
- **Scaling to complex environments**: AdaJEPA is evaluated only on 2D tasks (PushT, PointMaze); whether single-step self-supervised adaptation is sufficient for high-dimensional 3D environments with richer dynamics is untested
- **Cross-episode knowledge accumulation**: AdaJEPA resets to the pretrained model each episode; whether adaptation can safely accumulate across episodes without catastrophic forgetting or drift is open
- **Semantic vs. structural shifts**: Adaptation struggles with color shifts that change semantically meaningful features (anchor vs. object identity); combining test-time adaptation with invariance learning or data augmentation is unexplored
- **Integration with policy learning**: AdaJEPA adapts only the world model during MPC planning; whether jointly adapting world model and policy at test time improves or destabilizes performance is unknown
- **Adaptation beyond JEPA**: Whether the plan–execute–adapt–replan loop transfers to other world model architectures (RSSMs, diffusion-based, autoregressive) is open

**World reasoning benchmarks (VBVR):**
- **Real-world generalization**: VBVR tasks are curated/synthetic; robustness on in-the-wild video reasoning not extensively evaluated
- **Long-horizon reasoning**: Tasks emphasize short-range reasoning; multi-step causal chains over extended video remain underexplored
- **Model architecture diversity**: Scaling study focuses on generation models; how discriminative and other architectures scale is open
- **Task distribution bias**: Despite community contribution, coverage may be uneven; some capabilities may have fewer exemplars
- **Extrapolation beyond 2M**: Do scaling trends continue to 10B+ samples? Classical scaling law assumptions may not hold

**Data efficiency and scaling (BabyZWM):**
- **Scaling to larger datasets**: BabyZWM trains on 868 hours (single child); how does performance scale with multi-child data or larger datasets? Is there a plateau or continued improvement?
- **Generalization beyond child video**: BabyZWM trained on egocentric child video; robustness to other egocentric domains (robotics, adult POV, synthetic) unexplored
- **Complex real-world physics**: Evaluation focuses on simple object dynamics; soft bodies, friction, fluids, complex interactions not tested
- **True causal reasoning**: Approximate causal inference compares predictions; whether model supports true counterfactual interventions ("what if I push that?") is unclear
- **Scaling to richer semantics**: Model learns appearance/dynamics factorization; integration with semantic understanding (object categories, agent intentions) is open

**Integration:**
- **Physics ↔ Task reasoning**: Can V-JEPA's physics improve VBVR task performance? Do task-trained models develop emergent physics?
- **Joint evaluation**: No existing benchmark that simultaneously measures physics understanding and task reasoning
- **BabyZWM ↔ VBVR**: Does BabyZWM's data-efficient learning scale to VBVR's 200 tasks? Can zero-shot extraction rival VBVR's rule-based evaluation?
- **Data-efficient architectures for benchmarking**: Can BabyZWM's architectural principles (sparse factorization, causal inference) improve sample efficiency on VBVR?

## Contradictions and debates

- **Core knowledge vs. learned emergence**: Traditional developmental psychology argues physics is innate ("core knowledge"). V-JEPA's success suggests learned emergence, but may reflect specific inductive biases of the architecture rather than true "learning from scratch."
- **Explicit 3D vs. compressed latent representations**: SceneTok argues that unstructured tokens without 3D inductive bias outperform explicit 3D representations (Gaussians, NeRFs) for generation while maintaining NVS quality. This challenges the assumption that 3D structure is necessary for scene understanding and generation — but whether compressed tokens capture true 3D reasoning or just learn view-correlated statistics remains open.
- **Latent-space vs. 3D-space world models**: V-JEPA and DeltaWorld operate in learned representation space; PointWorld and TraceGen operate directly in 3D physical space with explicit geometric structure. PointWorld uses action-conditioned point flows while TraceGen uses embodiment-agnostic scene-level traces — both succeed for manipulation, but with different trade-offs (PointWorld: zero-shot MPC from many trajectories; TraceGen: few-shot warm-up from minimal demos). Whether latent, point-flow, and trace-space approaches can be unified — or represent fundamentally different trade-offs — is unresolved.
- **Frozen vs. adaptive world models**: Most world models (Dreamer, Genie, V-JEPA, PointWorld) are frozen at deployment, relying on training-time generalization. AdaJEPA argues the opposite: models should continuously adapt using self-supervised prediction error on observed transitions. PROWL takes a middle position — improving the frozen model via adversarial curriculum during training rather than at test time. Whether test-time adaptation (AdaJEPA) or training-time robustness (PROWL, data augmentation) is more effective — and whether they are complementary — is unresolved.
- **Derivative-free vs. gradient-based policy optimization in world models**: Ha & Schmidhuber use CMA-ES (derivative-free) for robustness to model errors; Dreamer uses analytic gradient propagation and achieves far stronger results. This suggests model accuracy has reached a threshold where gradients are useful rather than misleading — but whether this holds for more complex, partially observable real-world environments is untested.
- **Action-supervised vs. action-free world models**: Traditional world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, PointWorld) require paired action-state data, limiting them to environments with known action spaces. Genie demonstrates that unsupervised latent action discovery from passive video can produce controllable world models — but the discovered actions are discrete, low-dimensional (8 codes), and domain-specific. VPT occupies a middle ground, using an IDM trained on minimal labeled data to pseudo-label massive unlabeled video — requiring far less annotation than full action supervision but more than Genie's zero-label approach. DreamGen extends the IDM/latent-action paradigm to video world model outputs — generating videos without actions and recovering pseudo-actions post-hoc — achieving strong real-world manipulation results. Whether unsupervised approaches can match the precision and richness of action-supervised models for complex tasks (manipulation, long-horizon planning) remains open.
- **World models as planners vs. data generators**: Most work treats world models as real-time planners (Dreamer, AdaJEPA) or interactive environments (Genie, MineWorld, Yume1.5). DreamGen argues for the orthogonal role of world models as offline synthetic data generators — generating neural trajectories at scale for downstream policy training. The log-linear scaling and zero-to-one generalization results suggest this paradigm has significant room to grow. Whether planner and data-generator roles can be combined (e.g., using the same world model for both online planning and offline data augmentation) is unexplored.
- **Explicit dynamics models vs. implicit behavioral priors**: DreamerV3 and VPT both achieve diamond-level Minecraft results via fundamentally different mechanisms. DreamerV3 learns an explicit RSSM dynamics model and optimizes policies through imagined trajectories; VPT learns an implicit behavioral prior from human video and fine-tunes with RL. DreamerV3 requires no human data but massive environment interaction; VPT requires minimal labeled data but leverages ~70K hours of human demonstrations. Whether explicit dynamics models or implicit behavioral priors generalize better to novel tasks and environments — and whether they can be productively combined — is an open question.

## Recommended reading order

1. [[papers/2018/2018-world-models/2018-world-models|World Models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018)]] — the foundational paper establishing the V-M-C architecture, dream training, and stochastic latent dynamics; read first to understand the paradigm that all subsequent work builds upon
2. [[papers/2020/2020-dreamer/2020-dreamer|Dreamer (Hafner et al., ICLR 2020)]] — operationalizes dream training at scale with differentiable actor-critic learning in RSSM latent space; read after World Models to see how gradient-based imagination replaces derivative-free optimization
3. [[papers/2021/2021-dreamerv2/2021-dreamerv2|DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., ICLR 2021)]] — categorical latents and KL balancing close the model-based/model-free gap on Atari; read for the representation learning insights that made world models competitive on the hardest benchmarks
4. [[papers/2023/2023-dreamerv3/2023-dreamerv3|DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., Nature 2025)]] — scale-invariant robustness techniques enable domain-general RL with fixed hyperparameters across 150+ tasks; read for the completion of the Dreamer arc and Minecraft diamond from scratch
5. [[papers/2022/2022-vpt-learning-to-act-watching-videos/2022-vpt-learning-to-act-watching-videos|VPT (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022)]] — semi-supervised imitation learning from internet video via inverse dynamics; demonstrates that implicit world knowledge in behavioral priors enables diamond pickaxe crafting when fine-tuned with RL; read for the complementary path to Dreamer (implicit behavioral priors vs. explicit dynamics models)
6. [[papers/2023/2023-gaia-1-generative-world-model-autonomous-driving/2023-gaia-1-generative-world-model-autonomous-driving|GAIA-1 (Hu et al., 2023)]] — scales world modeling to autonomous driving via autoregressive next-token prediction over discretized video; demonstrates LLM-like scaling laws and emergent 3D understanding; bridges world models and generative video synthesis
7. [[papers/2024/2024-genie/2024-genie|Genie (Bruce et al., ICML 2024)]] — removes the action-label requirement: unsupervised latent action discovery from internet video creates a foundation world model that bridges video generation and interactive control
4. [[papers/2025-intuitive-physics-self-supervised-pretraining|V-JEPA Intuitive Physics (2025)]] — establishes that representation-space prediction learns physics without explicit supervision; entry point for understanding emergent world models from self-supervised learning
3. [[papers/2026-babyzwm-zero-shot-world-models|BabyZWM (2026)]] — demonstrates data-efficient learning and zero-shot transfer from naturalistic child video; shows how architectural choices enable both developmental plausibility and flexible inference; bridges self-supervised learning with cognitive science
4. [[papers/2026-vbvr-very-big-video-reasoning|VBVR (2026)]] — provides large-scale benchmarks to measure world reasoning capabilities and their scaling behavior; read last to see how diverse reasoning tasks measure progress from foundational physics to complex reasoning
   - **Alternative entry (evaluation-focused)**: Start with VBVR if interested in benchmarking rather than learning mechanisms
5. [[papers/2026-deltatok|DeltaTok / DeltaWorld (2026)]] — delta tokenization for efficient generative forecasting; read for the temporal compression perspective — how predicting change rather than state enables 2,000× compute reduction
6. [[papers/2026-scenetok|SceneTok (2026)]] — compressed scene tokenization for generative 3D; read for the spatial compression perspective on world models — how to compress and generate 3D environments efficiently
7. [[papers/2026/2026-pointworld/2026-pointworld|PointWorld (CVPR 2026)]] — action-conditioned 3D world model for robotic manipulation; read for the concrete instantiation of world models in physical interaction, scaling laws in 3D, and the unified point flow state-action representation
8. [[papers/2025/2025-dreamgen/2025-dreamgen|DreamGen (Jang et al., NVIDIA, 2025)]] — video world models as offline synthetic data generators for robot learning; read for the paradigm shift from world-models-as-planners to world-models-as-data-generators, and for the zero-to-one generalization results
9. [[papers/2026/2026-shareverse-collaborative-video-shared-world/2026-shareverse-collaborative-video-shared-world|ShareVerse (2026)]] — collaborative multi-agent video generation for shared world modeling; read for the extension of world models from single-observer to multi-agent shared reality via cross-agent attention and spatiotemporal memory
